# External-support re-verification pass — START (the six readings filed as independently supported)

Filed 2026-11-21 under the owner's order of that day (Glossa-Lab chat,
weekend iteration on the independent audit of the 94 strict-core
readings). The audit ranked primary-publication verification of the
six readings carrying outside-support claims among the evidence that
would most change the standings, and it was explicit about what such
verification can and cannot do: a verified prior proposal moves
provenance, not evidential strength; misattribution or lexical error
would remove claimed support.

This record is the START of that pass, not its completion. It covers
the six readings filed as independently supported — M044 ku,
M047 min/mīn, M124 kuTam, M202 can, M329 poṉ, M330 can — checked
against the primary publications reachable lawfully on 2026-11-21:

- Parpola 1994, *Deciphering the Indus Script* — the program's held
  copy, an OCR scan in restricted custody (recovered 2026-11-17;
  never committed to this repository). Searched as extracted text;
  the scan's OCR is imperfect, so a negative result below is stated
  as "not found in the held copy's text", with the search terms
  named, not as proof that no printed page contains a cognate form.
- Parpola 2010, *A Dravidian solution to the Indus script problem* —
  the program's held copy, byte-identical to its institutional
  repository source. Searched as extracted text.
- Mahadevan 2009, *Vestiges of Indus Civilisation in Old Tamil* —
  the lecture text as published by the Roja Muthiah Research Library,
  read in full text from its public posting.
- The Dravidian etymological dictionary entries named in the filed
  bases, checked through the University of Texas DravidiLex pilot,
  which reproduces the dictionary's second-edition text with page
  numbers, and through the dictionary's public digital edition.

No other primary publication named in the six filed bases was
lawfully reachable as text in this pass. In particular the codes
MC-18, MC-21, SL-04 and SL-14 in the filed bases are program-internal
pointers; the filed record does not expand them into publications,
and no Elamite or Sanskrit dictionary source for the forms they name
was retrieved. Where a leg of a filed basis could not be checked, it
is recorded as unverified, not as failed.

Outcomes use four labels only: VERIFIED AS FILED / PARTIALLY
VERIFIED / CONTRADICTED / NOT VERIFIABLE FROM LAWFULLY REACHABLE
MATERIAL. "Verified" here always means verified as a prior proposal
or as a dictionary entry — provenance. No outcome in this record
changes any standing; standings move only through the adjudication
record.

## M044 — ku

**Claim as filed.** Phase-276 dual external corroboration: Elamite
'kun' (MC-18) + Sanskrit 'kona' (SL-04) + DEDR 1638; three evidence
lines counted (crosswalk corpus, DEDR, external corroboration).

**What the reachable sources say.** No reachable primary source was
found proposing ku for this sign. Parpola 1994, the only named-author
work in the wider basis family, contains no 'kun' or 'kona' passage
offering a value for an Indus sign in the held copy's text (searches:
kun, kona, and the normalised full text). Sanskrit koṇa is a real
lexeme, 'corner, angle' (Monier-Williams, p. 313), and its standard
etymology treats it as borrowed from Dravidian (compare Tamil
kōṇam) — so even as a lexeme it is not an independent Sanskrit
witness standing apart from the Dravidian tradition. The Elamite
form 'kun' could not be verified from any reachable Elamite source,
and DEDR entry 1638 could not be retrieved by number from the
reachable dictionary reproductions in this pass.

**Outcome: NOT VERIFIABLE FROM LAWFULLY REACHABLE MATERIAL.** The
lexeme koṇa exists; nothing reachable connects it, or Elamite 'kun',
to this sign or to the value ku.

## M047 — min/mīn

**Claim as filed.** The distributional assignment 'par' was
overridden by crosswalk evidence: M047 = P47 = 'fish (plain)' per
the Mahadevan–Parpola crosswalk, sourced from Parpola 1994a +
Mahadevan; two evidence lines counted (crosswalk corpus, external
corroboration).

**What the primary source actually says.** Parpola 1994, chapter 10,
section 10.1 ('Fish = star: astral divinities', pp. 179–182 of the
held copy), proposes exactly this family of values for the fish
signs: "In most Dravidian languages the usual word for 'fish' is
mīn (Table 10.1). This phonetic shape can be reconstructed also for
the mother language, Proto-Dravidian. A homophone mīn denoting
'star' likewise occurs in many Dravidian languages and can be
reconstructed for Proto-Dravidian", with Table 10.1 setting out the
etyma *mīn 'fish' and *mīn 'star' across the Dravidian languages, and
the plain 'fish' sign identified pictorially at pp. 179–180. Parpola
2010 restates the same proposal in the same terms ("The most
commonly used word for 'fish' in Dravidian languages is mīn, and has
the homophone mīn meaning 'star'"). So a named outside author did
propose the filed value, for the fish-sign family that includes the
plain fish sign, in the named work.

**Limits.** What is verified is the proposal and its target family.
Parpola's argument is a rebus proposal for the fish signs as a
class; it does not single out this catalogue sign from its modified
fish-sign relatives, and the step from the crosswalk's sign identity
(M047 = the plain fish sign) to Parpola's class-level proposal is
the program's own step. Mahadevan's independent position could not
be checked from a reachable text in this pass: the crosswalk's
Mahadevan citation is incomplete as filed, and no Mahadevan text
proposing mīn for this sign was retrieved. Verification of a prior
proposal moves provenance, not evidential strength.

**Outcome: VERIFIED AS FILED** — as a prior proposal by the named
author in the named work, with the two limits above on the record.

## M124 — kuTam

**Claim as filed.** Gloss pot/jar; source Parpola_1994_via_phase51;
the same Phase-276 dual external corroboration as M044 (Elamite
'kun' MC-18 + Sanskrit 'kona' SL-04) + DEDR 1651; four evidence
lines counted.

**What the reachable sources say.** The dictionary leg checks out
as a lexeme: the etymological dictionary's entry reproduced at
second-edition p. 151 gives Tamil kuṭam 'waterpot', with cognates
across the family (Malayalam kuṭam 'waterpot', Kannada koḍa
'earthen pitcher or pot', Telugu kuḍaka 'cup, bowl', and others) —
the filed gloss pot/jar matches a real Dravidian word of the filed
shape. That is lexical availability only: the dictionary records a
word, not a reading of this sign. The Parpola attribution could not
be verified: no passage proposing kuTam, or a pot/jar value, for an
Indus sign was found in the held Parpola 1994 text, and the filed
source is a via-phase pointer, not a passage. The kun/kona leg is
the same unverified leg as M044, and as the audit noted, it does
not in any case account for the remaining segments of kuTam.

**Outcome: PARTIALLY VERIFIED** — the dictionary entry exists as
filed; the outside-proposal and external-corroboration legs are
unverified.

## M202 — can

**Claim as filed.** Phase-163 sibilant discovery: reading 'can'
found in literature, 4 mentions across sources:
Mahadevan_2009_vestiges; provides partial coverage for the
Shu-ilishu /su/ phonological slot; plus a truncated Phase-276 dual
external corroboration line.

**What the named source actually says.** The full text of
Mahadevan's 2009 lecture *Vestiges of Indus Civilisation in Old
Tamil* was read and searched. It contains no reading 'can' for any
Indus sign, no sibilant discussion, and no mention of Shu-ilishu or
of a /su/ slot. Its sign readings are other values entirely (among
them muruku for the intersecting-circles sign and the bearer-sign
interpretations). The word 'can' occurs in it only as the ordinary
English verb. Four mentions of one publication would in any case be
one source, not four.

**Outcome: CONTRADICTED.** The named source, in its published full
text, does not contain the filed claim. This is a finding about the
citation as filed; it does not by itself decide what value, if any,
this sign carries.

## M329 — poṉ

**Claim as filed.** Round 5 medial distributional assignment
(frequency 5); Phase-276 dual external corroboration: Elamite
'bur/pur' (MC-21) + Sanskrit 'pura' (SL-14) + DEDR 4524; gloss
'gold, precious; personal name'; four evidence lines counted.

**What the reachable sources say.** The dictionary leg checks out
as a lexeme: the entry reproduced at second-edition p. 404 gives
Tamil poṉ 'gold, metal, iron, wealth, ornament, beauty', Malayalam
pon 'gold', Kannada pon 'gold, metal', Telugu ponnu 'gold', and
cognates — the filed gloss 'gold, precious' matches. The filed
outside comparison does not support that gloss: Sanskrit pur /
pura is a real word, but it means 'stronghold, fortress, city,
town' (from the Indo-European root for a fortified place; cognate
with Greek polis), not gold or anything precious, and no reachable
source connects it to poṉ. The Elamite form 'bur/pur' could not be
verified from any reachable Elamite source, and no reachable
primary source was found proposing poṉ for this sign.

**Outcome: PARTIALLY VERIFIED** — the dictionary entry exists as
filed; the external-corroboration leg is unverified, and the one
part of it that could be checked (Sanskrit pura) does not carry
the filed gloss.

## M330 — can

**Claim as filed.** Phase-163 sibilant discovery: reading 'can'
found in literature, 4 mentions across sources: Parpola_1994;
provides partial coverage for the Shu-ilishu /su/ phonological
slot; plus a truncated Phase-276 dual external corroboration line
beginning 'Elamit…'.

**What the named source actually says.** The held Parpola 1994
text was searched in full, including a whitespace-normalised
search of the whole book. It contains no proposal of 'can' for any
Indus sign and no occurrence of Shu-ilishu. Its only sibilant
material is general phonology (palatal and dental sibilants in the
Dravidian/Indo-Aryan areal discussion, and an index entry), not a
sign reading. Because the held copy is an OCR scan, a negative
result from its text is weaker than the M202 result, which rests
on a clean published text: a short passage could in principle be
misread by the OCR in a way that hides a form. No such passage is
indicated by the book's contents, index, or any reachable secondary
account, and four mentions of the one book would in any case be
one source.

**Outcome: NOT VERIFIABLE FROM LAWFULLY REACHABLE MATERIAL** —
with the negative finding above on the record: the claim was
looked for in the named work and was not found there.

## Follow-on forms checked at lower depth

- **M044 / M124 — Sanskrit koṇa.** Real lexeme, 'corner, angle';
  standardly treated as a Dravidian borrowing into Sanskrit, so it
  cannot serve as a tradition independent of Dravidian even where
  it is cited alongside Dravidian material. Elamite 'kun': not
  verified from any reachable source.
- **M329 — Sanskrit pura.** Real lexeme, 'stronghold, city, town',
  of Indo-European origin. It does not mean gold, and no derivation
  from it to poṉ was found in any reachable source. Elamite
  'bur/pur': not verified from any reachable source.

5. The truncated corroboration lines in the M202 and M330 bases.
   As filed they are cut off mid-claim and cannot be checked until
