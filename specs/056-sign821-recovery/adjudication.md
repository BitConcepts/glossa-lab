## 5. Double derivation of the acceptance tests (doctrine record)

Per the autonomous-execution doctrine, each
claim's acceptance test was derived twice,
independently, from the requirement alone,
before the register was written:

- **Derivation A** worked from the
  requirement text forward: for each claim,
  what observable record, produced by the
  run itself, would show the requirement
  held?
- **Derivation B** worked from the falsifier
  and the pre-written outcome wordings
  backward: for each claim, what would a
  hostile reader check in the outcome record
  to show the requirement had *not* held?

The two derivations agreed on the gate,
checklist, and inspection tests for
F056-C02, C05, C06, C10, C12, C13, and C15
without amendment: in each case A's "record
produced in order" and B's "absence or
disorder of that record falsifies" are the
same check read from the two directions, and
the register states it once.

Disagreements, and their resolutions:

- **X-1 — What is "the passage"?** A bounded
  it at the anchor paragraph: the sentence
  the extraction pulled, in its paragraph.
  B held that the paragraph alone cannot be
  judged: the anchor sentence opens
  "Whatever sign 821 reads…", which
  presupposes a reading discussion standing
  before it, and A's bound would judge the
  passage by a slice as arbitrary as the
  original table cell — the extraction error
  repeated at paragraph scale.
  **Resolution:** B governs, one level up
  and no further: the headed section, the
  smallest self-contained discussion unit
  the document itself delimits (DD-056-1 /
  F056-C05). The whole-chapter bound A
  feared and B rejected is recorded as an
  alternative in the claim, refused for the
  mirror-image reason.
- **X-2 — What counts as "a specific
  argument"?** A counted any specific
  assertion about sign 821 in the passage —
  including a precise distributional
  observation (for example, an exact
  statement of the positions the sign
  occupies). B held that specificity is not
  the claim form: a precise observation with
  no statable falsification condition is the
  fragment's defect at greater length, and
  crediting it would let the act "recover"
  exactly what it was convened to test for.
  **Resolution:** B governs; the recovery
  test is conjunctive (R-1…R-5, F056-C08),
  and a specific-but-unfalsifiable candidate
  fails at R-4 with the failure named on its
  checklist — its specificity is recorded,
  not discarded.
- **X-3 — If the argument exists but the
  sign cannot be identified, is that "no
  claim recovered", hence retirement?** A,
  reading the design's binary literally,
  mapped it to RETIREMENT RECOMMENDED: the
  design's (a) requires the list named and
  the sign identified, so (a) fails, so (b).
  B quoted the retirement wording against
  A: (b)'s output calls the fragment "an
  extraction artefact" — a statement that
  the source made no argument — which would
  be false on A's own premise, and would
  bury a real argument under a record
  hygiene label. **Resolution:** B governs;
  the case closes as IDENTIFICATION FAILED,
  the family precedent (DD-056-2 /
  F056-C09), flagged OPEN-2. The campaign's
  binary is preserved for the *deciding*
  outcomes; a closure is a statement that
  the deciding act could not complete, in
  the campaign's own R-4 terms.
- **X-4 — One extraction pass or two?** A
  proposed a single executor pass with a
  complete sheet, noting the program's desk
  acts are usually single-pass. B held that
  this act's entire substance is one
  judgement call, and a single unrecorded
  reading makes the outcome uninspectable
  in exactly the place a reader needs to
  check — while two passes with a union set
  and quotation-settled reconciliation cost
  little and expose the judgement.
  **Resolution:** B governs (DD-056-4 /
  F056-C07), including B's tie-break:
  unsettled disagreement resolves *against*
  recovery, because recovery is the outcome
  that puts a new claim on the record.
- **X-5 — Where does a recovered claim
  live?** A proposed a new claim record
  through normal intake, on design D-19's
  pattern (recovered passages become their
  own claim records). B distinguished
  D-19: there, one heading pointed at up to
  five *different* findings in five
  documents, so new records are the only
  shape that fits; here, one fragment points
  at one passage's one argument, and D-17's
  text says the argument "is extracted as
  the claim" — the fragment's own record
  completed, not a new record beside an
  orphaned fragment. **Resolution:** B
  governs at draft (DD-056-3 / F056-C11);
  the choice is flagged OPEN-3 because
  claim-identifier discipline is the
  freeze's to confirm.
- **X-6 — What does retirement *do*?** A
  proposed that the execution withdraw the
  fragment — remove it from the claim set's
  live listings and mark the extracted
  record itself retired. B held that A
  reaches beyond the design ("a
  recommendation, recorded on the record, to
  retire the fragment… by an appended, dated
  disposition") and beyond append-only
  discipline: the design's instrument is the
  appended disposition, and an execution
  that edits the extracted record destroys
  the evidence of what was retired.
  **Resolution:** B governs; retirement is
  the enumerated appends and nothing else
  (DD-056-5 / F056-C12), with the reach
  across program surfaces carried as
  OPEN-6 rather than assumed.

## 6. Defects found at drafting

- **D-1 — Design D-17's outcome set is not
  total.** Its binary (recovered claim /
  retirement) maps no word onto the case
  where the passage argues something
  specific but the sign list cannot be named
  or the sign identified from the document —
  a live case here, since the program's
  registry carries no W821 entry. Repaired
  at draft by DD-056-2 (IDENTIFICATION
  FAILED closure, on family precedent),
  flagged OPEN-2; the design itself is not
  edited.
- **D-2 — The design does not bound "the
  source passage".** The same book argues
  about sign 821 in at least two other
  places (Chapter 3; the appendices), both
  verified at this draft. Unbounded
  re-extraction could import an argument
  from either and credit it to this
  fragment, "recovering" a claim its
  extraction never pointed at — or, in the
  other direction, an executor could bound
  the passage at the anchor sentence and
  retire a fragment whose section argues
  more. Repaired at draft by DD-056-1's
  mechanical bounds; flagged OPEN-1.
- **D-3 — The fragment's own record is
  truncated.** The claim record's
  `normalized_claim` and `quote_fragment`
  end mid-reference ("(Figure 6"). Any
  protocol that pinned the passage from the
  record's text would pin a cut-off quote.
  Repaired at draft by pinning from the
  source document (spec §2) and recording
  the truncation here; the record itself is
  not edited.
- **D-4 — The held cleaned text cannot
  carry the whole passage.** Sign glyphs
  are dropped (blank slots stand where
  glyphs were) and Figure 6.13 — the
  figure the anchor sentence cites for the
  sign's contexts — is present only as its
  caption. A contention carried only by a
  glyph or by the figure's images is
  invisible to a text-only re-extraction.
  Carried as a stated limit (spec §§2, 9)
  and as OPEN-4; not repaired at draft,
  because the repair (admitting figure
  inspection) changes the passage of record
  and is the freeze's decision.
- **D-5 — The identification route the
  design assumes may not exist.** Design
  (a) requires "the sign list named and the
  sign identified"; the program's crosswalk
  registry — the family's usual
  identification instrument — contains no
  W821 entry as held today, and its nearest
  look-alike (`Yunmapped_0821`) is a trap
  from a different numbering system. The
  draft repairs what it can (R-3's second
  route: the source list's own published
  entry, quoted with locator — the same
  fallback Spec 046 step 1 allows) and
  pre-writes the honest closure for the
  case where both routes fail (D-1).
- **D-6 — A tension between the design and
  the campaign, reconciled at draft.** The
  design's outcome (a) includes "a test is
  designed against it at that point"; the
  campaign's drafting instruction for this
  spec fixes that a recovered claim is NOT
  thereby tested and enters the lifecycle
  at its proper stage. Read together they
  are consistent — design at recovery, no
  run, entry at created — and DD-056-6
  records that reading. If the freeze reads
  the design as requiring more (a designed
  *and registered* test inside this spec),
  that is an OPEN-8 decision, not an
  executor's improvisation.

No defect was found that prevents stating
the falsifier, the step-0 gates, the
recovery test, or the pre-written wordings
for the full outcome set; the draft
therefore advances as a draft. Nothing in
this section amends design D-17, which
stands as written in the packet.
