## D. What the sustained set means for the paper

**The round-2 pattern is an echo problem, not a new-findings problem.** Of the 58 items, the great majority are places where a round-1 cure was applied in its home section and never carried to the places that repeat the claim: the abstract (R2-03, R2-10, R2-11, R2-51), the §1.3 summary list (R2-03, R2-12, R2-13, R2-14, R2-28, R2-30, R2-39, R2-56), and the summary sections §3.30, §4.5 and §5 (R2-03, R2-05, R2-15, R2-28, R2-40). A reader who reads the paper front to back — abstract and summary list first — meets the pre-cure strength first and the cure later, if at all. The lesson for the next revision pass is mechanical: every cure must be applied by searching the whole document for the claim, not by editing the section where the claim lives.

**Two round-1 replacement texts themselves contained errors.** Round 1's R-03 invented the parenthetical "0.001 is the smallest value a test of this size can report" (wrong — §C.2), and it was applied at three locations, so one drafting error became three printed errors (R2-10). What round 2 sustains is, in the main, three plainer defects: (1) claims printed at a strength the paper's own cured sections had already given up (Gulf "corroboration", V12 "artifact", "converging tests", the §4.5 verdict); (2) numbers a reader cannot recompute from the page — some because inputs were rounded or unnamed (z-score inputs, the χ² formula, the permutation parenthetical), some because the underlying counts were never printed (the 19 blockers, the 25 substrate signs, the Phase-169 items), and some because the counts do not exist (the CISI denominator, the 161-set site table); and (3) methods facts that were never recorded at all (the DEDR search space, the SA training corpus), where the only honest cure is to print the gap.
