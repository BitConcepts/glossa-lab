## 3. Doubly derived acceptance tests (doctrine record)

Per the autonomous-execution doctrine, each
claim's acceptance test was derived twice,
independently: **Derivation A** from the
requirement text of the claim alone, and
**Derivation B** from the design's falsifier,
gates, and campaign requirements (R-2, R-3)
without reference to A. Where A and B agreed,
the register carries the agreed test. Three
disagreements arose; each is recorded here
with its resolution, and the register follows
the resolution:

1. **F045-C03/C04 (the two hypotheses).**
   A derived one combined test ("PH beats P
   and kind beats suffix behaviour") from the
   design's DISCRIMINATED FOR HUNT wording;
   B derived two separate tests, because the
   design's falsifier is disjunctive — either
   arm failing defeats the hypothesis.
   **Resolution: B.** The claims are split
   (C03, C04) with separate verdict rules,
   and §5.4 requires both arms in Hunt's
   favour for DISCRIMINATED FOR HUNT, both
   against for the grammar word, and anything
   else NOT DISCRIMINATED. A combined claim
   would have hidden which arm failed.
2. **F045-C09 (disputed signs).** A, from the
   claim text alone, resolved disputes by the
   §2.3 source hierarchy (CISI governs) so
   every sign keeps a kind; B, from the
   packet's own sensitivity practice (D-06,
   D-13) and the circularity control,
   excluded contested signs from the primary.
   **Resolution: B, with A's rule preserved
   as the sensitivity's inclusion rule**
   (CISI-governed label, §5.3). Hierarchy
   resolution in the primary would have
   manufactured agreement at exactly the
   signs where the identification layer is
   weakest.
3. **F045-C04's suffix measure.** A imported
   the measure from D-08 ("D-08's frozen
   closing-sign behaviour"), as the design's
   parenthetical names it; B objected that
   D-08 has not frozen or executed at this
   draft, so importing it would make this
   spec's test depend on an artefact that
   does not exist. **Resolution: B.** Spec
   §5.2 defines the measure operationally
   (distinct-predecessor rate in final
   position), and the freeze reconciles the
   definition against D-08's frozen text if
   D-08 has by then frozen (DD-045-4). This
   is recorded as a defect found in the
   design's wording (§5), not silently
   repaired.

## 5. Defects and ambiguities found in the design, and their handling

1. **D-08 dependency that does not yet
   exist.** D-05's hypothesis names the
   grammar predictor as "measured by D-08's
   frozen closing-sign behaviour"; D-08 is
   itself only a design at this draft.
   Handled by DD-045-4 (operational
   definition in §5.2, reconciliation at
   freeze). Not a drafting failure: the
   design's own closing-test paragraph
   defines the measure independently ("the
   rate at which it follows many different
   signs as a final element"), so the spec
   stands on the design's substance.
2. **"Jurisdiction signs" have no kind.**
   D-05's hypothesis assigns the closing part
   "cycle/sky and jurisdiction signs", but
   the design's kind list (animal, sky/cycle,
   object/goods, human, other) has no
   jurisdiction kind. Handled in §5.2(b):
   the jurisdiction subset is coded at entry
   from the verbatim published label, with
   the coding rule stated in the build
   record before the freeze. **Flagged
   OPEN-for-freeze (§6):** if the freeze
   judges entry-time coding too loose, the
   alternative is to restrict §5.2(b) to
   `sky_cycle` alone and record the
   narrowing openly.
3. **The kind list and the corpus's sign
   identity are under-specified in the
   design.** D-05 does not name the sign
   numbering its classification uses.
   Handled by §2.1 (the pinned crosswalk
   identity, with CISI and Parpola numbers
   carried per row). No substance changed:
   the crosswalk is the program's own
   identity record for exactly this purpose.
4. **No defect in the falsifier.** The
   falsifier, the three outcome words, and
   the freeze-ordering of the fit margin are
   all stated in the design; this draft did
   not have to invent any of them.
