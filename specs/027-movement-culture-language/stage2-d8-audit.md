## Stage 1 — Law (2011)

**Identity.** Randall W. Law, *Inter-Regional
Interaction and Urbanism in the Ancient Indus Valley:
A Geological Provenience Study of Harappa's Rock and
Mineral Assemblage* — Occasional Paper 11
(Linguistics, Archaeology and the Human Past), Indus
Project, Research Institute for Humanity and Nature,
Kyoto, 2011; Indian edition as *Current Studies on
the Indus Civilization*, Vol. VIII (Manohar, 2011),
in two parts (Part 1: Text; Part 2: Appendices and
References). The work rests on the author's
University of Wisconsin–Madison dissertation
research with the Harappa Archaeological Research
Project.

**What it holds, for link purposes.** Provenience
determinations tying artifacts to geologic source
areas, organised by material (chert, steatite, agate,
vesuvianite-grossular, alabaster, limestone, metals).
The author's own summary counts **more than 2,100
determinations for artifacts from Harappa and almost
120 for artifacts from other sites**. Methods are
recorded per material study: instrumental neutron
activation analysis for the agate and
vesuvianite-grossular studies (analyses at the
University of Wisconsin nuclear reactor facility),
strontium and sulfur isotope analysis for alabaster,
and — for the assemblage-wide provenience outlines
of Chapter 4 — assignment from field observation and
contemporary geologic reference materials.

**Availability and terms.** The book's chapters are
openly posted as PDFs on harappa.com, the project
site the author is associated with, including the
summary chapters where the determinations are
combined. No open licence is stated on the postings,
and no bulk machine-readable deposit of the
determinations was located (this audit searched for
a tDAR, Open Context, or Zenodo deposit and for any
stable download; none was found). Spec 027 §4's
blocker note — "no verified stable bulk-open
download for Law (2011) provenance data" — is
**re-confirmed as of 2026-10-12**. The artifact-level
determination tables live in the printed appendices
(Part 2).

**Posture: `open_access_read_only`.** Readable; bulk
reuse not verified. Lawful consequence under the
program's discipline: the published determinations
may be digitised for **internal** link construction
(the Phase-124 read-only precedent), and nothing
derived from them is released except at the audited
posture under adopted Ask 6A.

**Margins the Q7 freeze must carry.** (i) *Site
concentration*: roughly 95% of the determinations
are for a single site (Harappa); the multi-site link
mass is the ~120 other-site determinations plus the
per-site assemblage assignments. (ii) *Method mix*:
under adopted Ask 2B, only the instrumentally
analysed subsets enter the primary link tier; the
Chapter 4 reference-based assignments are the visual
tier and belong to the labelled sensitivity.

> **Stage 1 outcome: NOT a rights failure — proceed.**
> The largest D8 component is lawfully usable at
> read-only posture for internal link construction,
> with its method recorded per material study. The
> two margins above go forward to the freeze, where
> adopted Ask 5A fixes minimum-support rules from
> them.

## Stage 2 — Kenoyer/Vidale craft studies

**Identity.** The craft-technique classification
literature anchored by Kenoyer & Vidale 1992, "A new
look at stone drills of the Indus Valley Tradition"
(*Materials Issues in Art and Archaeology III*,
pp. 495–518), which defines the bead-drilling
technique classes from perforation morphology and
controlled experiments — pecked perforation, tapered
cylindrical drills, constricted cylindrical drills
(the "Ernestite" drill, made from a specific hard
stone), and copper drills used with abrasive (solid
and tubular) — with published experimental drilling
rates. The classification is extended and applied in
Kenoyer's subsequent bead-technology work (1997,
*World Archaeology* 29(2), on Harappa's drill types
and craft areas; 1998; 2000; 2017, "History of stone
beads and drilling: South Asia") and in Vidale's
production studies (steatite disc-bead manufacture
at Mohenjo-Daro and Mehrgarh, 1989/1995; craft
production studies with Miller). Later literature
applies the same classes to assemblages beyond the
Indus (e.g. Indus-style beads at Aegina-Kolonna).

**What it holds, for response purposes.** A published
classification scheme with diagnostic criteria
(drill-tip and perforation morphology, taper and
straightness measurement, SEM trace evidence) — the
bead/craft arm's response variable under adopted Ask
3A. It does **not** exist as a consolidated
per-object dataset: per-object classifications live
scattered across per-site and per-study publications
(Harappa, Chanhu-Daro, and Mohenjo-Daro assemblages
dominate), each with its classification source
recorded in that publication.

**Availability and terms.** The 1992 study and
Kenoyer's bead-technology summaries are openly
posted as author PDFs on harappa.com; the journal
and book versions are publisher-controlled. No open
licence is stated, and no consolidated dataset
deposit was located.

**Posture: `open_access_read_only`** for the openly
posted texts; **`catalogue_only`** for items
available only through publishers until they are
read under the same rule at freeze time.

**Margins the Q7 freeze must carry.** Coverage is a
per-study patchwork, not a designed cross-site
sample; under adopted Ask 3A each site enters the
bead/craft arm only if that arm's frozen
minimum-support rule is met on the published
classifications, with provenance method and
classification source recorded per similarity cell.

> **Stage 2 outcome: NOT a rights failure — proceed.**
> The bead/craft arm's classification basis is
> lawfully readable and traceable to published
> applications. The arm's dataset is assembled at
> the Q7 freeze from those applications; nothing in
> this stage requires a weaker substitute source.

## Stage 3 — Meluhha compilations

**Identity.** Possehl 1996, "Meluhha", in *The
Indian Ocean in Antiquity* (ed. Reade),
pp. 133–208 — a compilation of **76 cuneiform
citations** of Meluhha, from the Early Dynastic
period (c. 2500 BCE) to Isin-Larsa (c. 1900–1800
BCE), with the imported items itemised (Meluhha
woods, carnelian, lapis lazuli, pearls, copper,
gold, ivory and ivory objects, animals, and
Meluhhan-style objects and ships).

**What it holds, for link purposes.** Nothing at
site-pair level. The texts name a source **region**
— Meluhha, the greater Indus — never an Indus site
from which a material came. The compilation
therefore cannot contribute provenance links between
Indus site pairs under any posture; its evidentiary
role is contextual (which material classes moved
between the greater Indus and Mesopotamia), as the
adjudication packet anticipated when it sequenced
this component last.

**Availability and terms.** A publisher book
chapter; no open edition and no dataset was
located.

**Posture: `catalogue_only`.**

> **Stage 3 outcome: component closed as catalogued
> context; contributes no links.** This is not a
> rights failure of the link set — the component was
> never link-bearing. Q7's link basis stands or falls
> on Stages 1–2, and both cleared.

## Audit conclusion

The D8 audit is **complete**, and with it the §8.1
gate condition is met: Stage 0 is complete and this
audit establishes which provenance links are
lawfully and traceably usable. **No stage returned a
rights failure.** The lawful basis on which a Q7
freeze may now be designed is:

- **Links (primary tier, adopted Ask 2B):** Law
  (2011)'s instrumentally analysed determinations
  (INAA; Sr/S isotopes), digitised internally at
  `open_access_read_only`, never released (Ask 6A).
- **Links (sensitivity tier):** Law's Chapter 4
  reference-based assignments, separately labelled.
- **Bead/craft response (adopted Ask 3A):** the
  Kenoyer/Vidale published classifications and their
  published applications, at `open_access_read_only`,
  assembled per site with method and classification
  source recorded per cell.
- **Context only:** the Possehl Meluhha compilation,
  `catalogue_only`; no links.

**What this audit does not do.** It designs no Q7
freeze and freezes nothing: the Q7 freeze — its
minimum-support rules fixed from the margins
recorded above, under adopted Asks 2B, 3A, and 5A,
with the OSF registration prescribed by Ask 6A —
is a separate later act. It also digitises nothing;
digitisation begins only under that freeze.

**Residuals recorded for the freeze.** (i) *Ceramic
arm sources*: adopted Ask 3A builds the ceramic
response from published fabric/typology
classifications in site reports generally; those
reports are outside D8's three audited components,
and their posture is assigned under §5.1 at freeze
time — this audit clears nothing about them.
(ii) *Appendix-level field completeness*: Stage 1's
traceability finding rests on the openly posted
chapter texts and summaries; whether every printed
appendix table records method per determination is
re-verified at digitisation design time, before any
row enters a link set. (iii) *Deposit negative*: the
absence of a bulk-open Law dataset is stated as of
this audit's searches on 2026-10-12, not as a
timeless fact.
