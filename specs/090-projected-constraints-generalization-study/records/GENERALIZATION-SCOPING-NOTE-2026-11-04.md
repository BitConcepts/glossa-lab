# Deliverable 5 — Generalizing Projected Constraints: a scoping note
Status: scoping note, not a build, not a claim. Written 2026-11-04
under the owner's direction that the framework itself can be
generalized. Everything below separates what is established from
what is proposed, in the framework's own idiom.

## The question
Projected Constraints today is a physics framework: a candidate
foundational structure for systems described by order and
counting, with one primitive scale, necessity theorems, and
instantiations aimed at gravity and quantum dynamics. The owner's
question: is there a domain-independent skeleton inside it, of
which the physics is one instantiation, and what would prove that
rather than merely suggest it?

## The candidate skeleton (proposed, not established)
Strip the physics and five elements remain, in order:
1. RECORDS. A domain is described by a set of partial records
   (observations, reports, measurements, assertions), none of
   which is the system itself. In physics: causal-set elements
   and counting data. Elsewhere: ledger entries, anchors,
   sensor reports.
2. PROJECTION. A record is a projection of the system: it carries
   some structure and discards the rest, by a stated rule. The
   discipline's core question is always the same: what is
   invariant across all projections consistent with the records,
   and what is underdetermined by them?
3. ONE PRIMITIVE SCALE. Exactly one dimensionful (or otherwise
   absolute) input is admitted as primitive; everything else must
   be dimensionless relative to it, derived, or labeled
   unidentifiable in principle. In physics: the founding axiom
   (only rho R^2 is identifiable from order plus counting). The
   general form: name your one absolute, or prove you need none.
4. SEPARATORS AND NECESSARY CONDITIONS. The machinery generates
   necessary conditions: statements that must hold in every
   completion of the records (the F0 pattern: invariance and
   no-estimator theorems), and separators: quantities or tests
   that partition possible systems into classes the records can
   distinguish. A separator counting law, in physics, counts how
   much distinguishing power the records carry; the general form
   asks the same of any record system.
5. THE EXECUTABLE MATRIX. Records, claims about them, and their
   relations form a generated, gated, executable matrix (the
   winning combination's spine): implications are computed,
   verdicts are issued by machinery against frozen criteria, and
   corrections are append-only. This is the element VEXA already
   generalizes in practice.

## What is physics-specific today (honest boundaries)
- The arena: causal sets as the candidate record structure is a
  physics hypothesis, currently assumed as a candidate, not
  established, and the framework's own framing rule says no
  candidate is assumed true.
- The scale: the primitive scale's identity (a length, tied to
  the E22 degeneracy rho R^2) is physics. Other domains would
  carry a different absolute, or none, and the skeleton must
  survive that substitution formally, not rhetorically.
- The theorems: F0's results are proved about order-and-count
  observables on a scale group. Their general analogues (what an
  "order-and-count observable" is for, say, texts or markets)
  are not defined, let alone proved.
- The instantiations: gravity and quantum-dynamics bridges,
  anesthesia and cognition applications, and the separator
  constants computed so far are domain work. None of it transfers
  as evidence to another domain; only the machinery may transfer.

## What a second instantiation would have to show
Generalization is a claim, and it needs a falsifier. A proposed
second instantiation (a domain that is not physics and not a
metaphor for physics) counts as generalization, not analogy, only
if it delivers ALL of the following:
G1. Its records, projection rule, and (if any) primitive scale
    are defined in writing before any result, with the same
    rigor as the physics charter, including what its records
    provably cannot identify.
G2. At least one necessary condition is derived in that domain
    by the skeleton's machinery (not imported from physics), and
    stated with its falsifier.
G3. At least one separator is computed in that domain and shown
    to distinguish cases the domain's practitioners agree are
    distinct, on data not used to design the separator.
G4. A claim that the skeleton forbids is actually refused or
    killed by the domain's own gate at least once (the machinery
    must bite in the new domain, not only nod).
G5. An independent reader, given only the skeleton documents,
    can reproduce the domain's matrix from its records.
If any of G1 to G5 fails, the correct verdict is analogy, and
the honest product is a pattern note saying so, not a softened
claim of generality.

## Candidate second domains (assessment, not commission)
- Glossa-Lab / decipherment is the strongest near candidate, and
  the note must be careful: Glossa already runs the executable
  matrix and the ledger discipline, and its anchors are records
  with sources and coverage. What it has NOT done is state a
  projection rule, a primitive absolute, or a necessary condition
  in the skeleton's terms (its Spec 076/078 projected-constraint
  corpus work is adjacent, on its own terms). G1 and G2 are open
  there, which makes Glossa the honest test bed: close enough to
  attempt, distinct enough (texts, not spacetime) that success
  would mean something.
- The Breaking-Point Atlas is a projected-constraint system by
  its own charter (loads, thresholds, breaking points named in
  advance), but its "scale" and separator notions are informal.
  It could attempt G1 cheaply; G3 would be its real test.
- Workforce and solver applications (Axiovex's projected
  constraint solver for employers and universities) use constraint
  projection as engineering, without records/projection theorems;
  they are consumers of the name's intuition, not instantiations,
  and this note recommends against counting them.

## Proposed program, if the owner commissions it
Step 1: a skeleton document, written once, stating elements 1 to
5 formally enough that G1 to G5 can be judged against it, with
the physics instantiation as its appendix, not its body.
Step 2: Glossa attempts G1 and G2 as a registered study in its
own program (its chat owns the work; this chat supplies the
skeleton and the judging criteria).
Step 3: verdict from the records: generalization, analogy, or
the skeleton revised once and retested. Any of the three is a
publishable, honest outcome; only the first earns the word.
This note establishes none of that. It scopes the question,
names the bar, and waits for a commission.
