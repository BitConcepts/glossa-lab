# Decision record — 2026-10-25: Spec 071's sky-signs endings question retired as untestable in this programme (Candidate C)

## The decision

On 2026-10-25 the owner, Tristen Pierson, approved Candidate C of the
Spec 071 redesign proposal of 2026-10-24 ("Yes let's go with your
recommendation", 08:57 EDT), the recommendation having been
re-presented in the realignment completion report of the same date.
Candidate B (the depicted-kind endings atlas) was **not** approved and
is not part of this act.

**The question Spec 071 asked — whether the sky signs gather at the
ends of inscriptions, claim
`without_kings_or_conquests_the_indus_scr_ce9d98cc_manual_004` — is
RETIRED as untestable in this programme.** The claim stands
**UNTESTED**: no verdict, no status promotion of any kind. This record
and one dated append to the claim record are the whole act. There is
no new spec, no freeze, no OSF filing, and no execution, and Spec
071's and Spec 073's closures stand unamended.

## The grounds — measured, and already on the merged record twice

1. Spec 071 (Phase-434) froze the test on 2026-10-22 and closed
   **INVALID at gate 0.3 (coverage)**: only 285 of the 557 signs in
   the primary population carried an Asset A row under the frozen
   keying route. No final-position quantity was computed; the claim
   stands UNTESTED. The outcome record stated the adjacent fact at
   the time: Asset A v1's celestial class was already empty
   (0 of 187 rows).
2. Spec 073 (Phase-436, REPAIRED WITH NAMED SHORTFALL) repaired the
   coverage defect from published sources only. The 557-sign
   documented set now keys **556 of 557** (the sole remainder is
   token 999, a corpus placeholder appearing in no published sign
   list). A successor would pass gate 0.3.
3. The celestial class is empty anyway. Asset A v2 (552 rows:
   other 246, unidentified 211, human 43, animal 30,
   object_goods 22) carries **zero** `sky_cycle` rows, because no
   ruled published source depicts any keyed sign as a sky object.
   The sources consulted in Spec 073's build — CISI 3.1, the Fuls
   2023 *Catalog* and *Corpus*, Parpola 2010 — are exhausted on
   this point.
4. The verbatim labels beneath the kind column were checked for
   this closure. Across all 552 rows, exactly three labels contain
   a celestial word, and all three describe ray or sunburst
   *features of another depicted object* (each coded `other` from
   CISI, none disputed). None is a published identification of a
   sign as a sun, star, or moon, and Spec 071 §4.1 excluded exactly
   this construction in advance: a sign whose label a reader might
   call celestial while its kind is not `sky_cycle` is not a
   member.

## The closing finding

> The celestial class is empty in every classification this
> programme can honestly build. Asset A v1 (187 rows) and Asset A
> v2 (552 rows), both built from published sources only, entered
> blind, the second on the repaired keying surface that keys 556 of
> the 557 signs the documented corpus uses, each contain zero

> keyed sign as a sky object. The verbatim labels beneath the kind
> column were checked for this closure: across 552 rows, the only
> celestial words in any published label describe ray or sunburst
> features of other depicted objects, in three rows, none an
> identification of a sign as a celestial body. A class cannot be
> tested at a floor of 30 occurrences when it has no members. The
> claim stands UNTESTED, and the question is retired in this
> programme — not falsified, not supported: untestable here,
> because the published-identification layer the programme's
> discipline requires does not contain the class the question is
> about.

The empty celestial class is the line's closing finding.

## The governed reopening trigger

The retirement is a governed state, not a shrug. The line reopens
if and only if a published sign list that actually identifies
signs as celestial becomes available to the programme **and is
exhibited** — its labels inspectable in full, as Spec 073's gate
0.3 required of its sources. The two standing named candidates are
the two sources struck at Spec 073's freeze for non-exhibition:
**Mahadevan 1977** and **Wells 1998**.

If either is ever acquired for any reason, a bounded census —
count its celestial identifications against Registry v2, with
occurrences in the primary population — decides whether a
classification build is warranted: proceed only if the census
finds **at least 5 celestially identified signs totalling at
least 30 primary-population occurrences** (Spec 071's floor);
otherwise the closure stands and the census is appended to it.

The record contains **no evidence that either source depicts
celestial signs**; the trigger names them because they are the
programme's known unexamined published lists, not because they
are promising.

**Until that trigger fires, no successor spec, re-freeze, or
re-test of this question is to be proposed.**

## The honesty condition

A retirement has no falsifier in the test sense; its honesty
condition is the trigger above. If a ruled source already in the
programme's hands is shown to identify a sign as celestial — a
row this closure's label check missed — the closure is wrong on
its own terms and must be corrected by dated append, with the row
named.

## What this act does not establish

This act does not establish that Hunt's claim is false, that no
celestial signs exist in the script, or that the published lists
are complete — and it establishes nothing about sky signs,
calendars, cycles, ritual, or any sign's meaning. It establishes
the question's formal closing state in this programme and the
condition under which it reopens, on the measured ground that the
programme's own ruled sources, exhausted across two classification
builds, do not contain the class the question is about — and that
the programme will not manufacture one.
