# T303 — Identifiability Protocol, First Application (2026-11-16)

Spec 095 Stage 3, claim C095-9. The protocol is stated at
glossa-indus/STANDARDS.md, Part III (Standards of Record), where
the NOT IDENTIFIABLE vocabulary amendment is also recorded. This
record is the protocol's first application: rival constructions
against the six independently supported anchors of record (M044,
M047, M124, M202, M329, M330), the per-sign outcomes, and the six
standings re-stated under those outcomes.

## The evidence structure all six applications share

The six standings rest on the WP5 support audit
(docs/repair-2026-10-26/anchor-support-audit.json), which classifies
each as `supported_independent`: their support lines are external
in lineage. Three facts of that record frame every application:

1. No strict anchor record states a falsifier or a distinguishing
   observation (the audit records "no falsifier recorded" and
   "not recorded" for all six; GR-088-12 counts 94 of 94). Under
   Part III rule 1, identifiability requires observations the
   claim fits and the rivals do not; the record names none.
2. The Phase-48 evidence tests of record are weaker than their
   names suggest, on the audit's own code reading: the positional
   test never uses the reading argument (it tests distribution
   and functional role only), positional expectation passes by
   default when a role has no expectation, and the DEDR test
   passes a reading shorter than two characters outright and
   otherwise counts Dravidian dictionary entries equal to or
   prefix-matching the reading — a plausibility measure that many
   rival values pass with their own entry sets.
3. The Phase-276 "dual external corroboration" is, per sign, one
   Elamite and one Sanskrit comparandum, each a phonetic
   similarity judgement without stated sound correspondences.
   Similarity is satisfiable by many strings; it corroborates
   that a value is pronounceable in the region's languages, it
   does not identify it.

Each sign is nevertheless examined on its own record below; the
outcomes are uniform because the shared structure is what fails,
and the two partial discriminations the record does contain
(M047's meaning line, M202's sibilant slot) are recorded as such.

## M044 — reading ku (HIGH; n = 19 in the Phase-107 table)

Record: Phase-276 Elamite 'kun' (MC-18) + Sanskrit 'kona' (SL-04)
+ DEDR 1638 (audit and Phase-108 trail; the LEDGER.md Phase-83
passage records DEDR 1715 for this sign — the two records differ,
and neither states a distinguishing observation). The recorded
alternatives hi/la are Phase-107-falsified SA outputs, not rival
constructions.

Rival function: a vessel or measure logogram. The sign depicts a
jar; a logographic construction carries no phonetic value at all,
and no recorded observation distinguishes it from a syllabogram —
the distributional evidence is value-blind (shared fact 2) and
the counts are 19.

Rival value: another Dravidian syllable of the same shape (ka,
ko) passes the DEDR prefix test on its own entries; and the
comparanda pair kun/kona is also M124's recorded corroboration,
so the comparanda do not discriminate between signs, let alone
between values for this sign.

Outcome: NOT IDENTIFIABLE.

## M047 — reading min/mīn (HIGH; n = 13)

Record: the 2026-05-11 fact-check correction — M047 = P47 'fish
(plain)' per the Mahadevan–Parpola crosswalk — confirmed by
iconographic_anchors.json (fish-in-crocodile-mouth; H-902B
offerings tablet; eZu-miin compound); DEDR 4897 (fish/star);
Phase-276 Elamite 'in' (MC-01) + Sanskrit 'mina' (SL-06). An
earlier distributional value 'pār' (LOW) was overridden by the
crosswalk line.

Component analysis. The *meaning* 'fish' carries the strongest
discriminating line of the six: the drawing test and the
iconographic contexts are observations a rival meaning must also
explain, and the recorded rival gloss 'star' (the same Dravidian
word's second gloss) does not explain the fish depiction or the
crocodile tablet. The *phonetic value* mīn is a further step: it
follows only by the rebus assumption — that the sign carries the
sound of the Dravidian word for the depicted object. Rival
constructions the record does not exclude: the sign as a FISH
logogram with no phonetic value; the sign carrying the fish-word
of another contact-zone language. The Sanskrit comparandum
'mina' is itself plausibly a Dravidian loanword, which would make
that corroboration line same-lineage; this is recorded as a
caveat on the line, not as a settled finding.

Outcome: the reading (value mīn) NOT IDENTIFIABLE; the meaning
component 'fish' is the one component of the six applications
with a discriminating observation of record, and the re-statement
says so.

## M124 — reading kuTam, gloss 'pot / jar' (HIGH; n = 3)

Record: Parpola 1994 via phase51; DEDR 1651; Phase-276 Elamite
'kun' (MC-18) + Sanskrit 'kona' (SL-04) — the identical comparanda
pair recorded for M044.

Rival constructions: at n = 3 no distributional observation
distinguishes any function from any other. The comparanda,
shared with M044, cannot identify this sign's distinct longer
value: the rival value 'kun' — the Elamite comparandum itself,
read as a contact form — fits the comparanda at least as directly
as kuTam does, and a vessel logogram (the gloss is 'pot / jar')
fits the iconography. No observation in the record prices the
value's second syllable at all.

Outcome: NOT IDENTIFIABLE.

## M202 — reading can (HIGH; n = 4)

Record: Phase-163 sibilant discovery (literature: Mahadevan 2009
vestiges, four mentions); partial coverage for the Shu-ilishu
/su/ slot; Phase-276 Elamite 'an' (MC-07) + Sanskrit 'annam/anna'
(SL-03). The crosswalk trail entry for this sign carries a blank
mahadevan_id join key — its literature line is weaker than its
standing suggests.

Component analysis. The Shu-ilishu slot is a genuine positional
constraint: it discriminates a sibilant value from non-sibilant
rivals for this sign in that context. It carries neither the
vowel nor the final nasal: rival values sa, śa, ṣa — sibilant
syllables of the same and of unrelated families — fit the slot
evidence equally, and the '-an' of the reading rests on the
comparanda alone (shared fact 3).

Outcome: the reading 'can' NOT IDENTIFIABLE; the sibilant class
is a discriminated component, recorded as such.

## M329 — reading poṇ, gloss 'gold, precious; personal name' (HIGH; n = 5)

Record: Round 5 distributional origin (MEDIAL position, freq 5);
DEDR 4524; Phase-276 Elamite 'bur/pur' (MC-21) + Sanskrit 'pura'
(SL-14). No Parpola crosswalk entry in the trail.

Rival constructions: the recorded corroboration is the weakest
of the six — the Elamite comparandum does not share the claimed
value's final consonant (r against ṇ), and the Sanskrit
comparandum's gloss ('city') does not match the claimed gloss.
A medial-only profile at n = 5 fits a grammatical morpheme or a
name logogram as well as a syllabogram, and the rival value 'pur'
(the comparandum itself) fits the comparanda at least as well as
poṇ does.

Outcome: NOT IDENTIFIABLE.

## M330 — reading can (HIGH; n = 3)

Record: Phase-163 sibilant (Parpola 1994); Phase-276 Elamite 'an'
(MC-07) + Sanskrit 'annam/anna' (SL-03) — the same evidence
package as M202. No Parpola crosswalk entry in the trail; the
trail entry's provenance fields are stale.

Rival construction: M202 itself. Two distinct signs carry the
same reading on the same evidence; homophony is possible (WP8
control family C4 exists to price exactly that flexibility), but
the recorded evidence does not distinguish "both signs read can"
from "M202 reads can and M330 reads another sibilant", or the
reverse. At n = 3 no distributional observation discriminates.

Outcome: NOT IDENTIFIABLE.

## Filed, not applied to the ledger — the mechanical reason

The stage brief directs the re-statement through the ledger's
append-only act machinery if the machinery can carry it with the
gate green. It cannot, demonstrably:

1. The ledger's act for changing a standing is the
   `reclassification` entry, and the intake validator refuses one
   whose `supersedes` names no ledger entry
   (backend/glossa_lab/ledger_intake.py, `validate_ledger`: a
   reclassification naming no entry is a validation error, and V1
   of the gate runs that validator).
2. No ledger entry embodies any of the six standings. Anchor
   readings are not ledger claims: the ledger's three sign_refs
   in total (GR-088-11) resolve to none of the six signs, and no
   claim entry states an anchor reading. The standings live in
   the WP5 audit (a frozen record; not editable) and in the
   canonical anchor table's confidence fields (gate-checked
   inputs whose edit is a data change, not an act: it would move
   the gate's V6 strict-set derivation and the 287-anchor
   census, and Spec 094's closeout verified that file
   byte-identical as a boundary of record).
3. Minting six new claim entries to serve as reclassification
   targets would create six new claims — census 994 to 1000,
   with no source of record — which is a different act from
   re-stating standings and outside T303's text.

The application is therefore filed complete in this record, the
ledger is untouched, the claim census is unchanged (1,025 lines,
994 claims), and the gate holds at the standing profile. The
re-statements above are the program's filed position under the
protocol. Carrying anchor standings as acts in a machine store
needs a vehicle that does not yet exist; it is named as a
remainder for the Spec 095 Stage 5 closeout's register re-check,
not silently dropped.
