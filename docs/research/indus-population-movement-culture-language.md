# Population Movement and Human Mobility in Relation to Culture and Language in the Greater Indus World — Before, During, and After the Indus Valley / Harappan Civilization

*A falsifier-first, source-critical study structured for a spec-kit + Applied Epistemic Engineering (AEE) ledger*

## Summary

**Strongest defensible conclusion, in one paragraph:** People, genes, goods, technologies, practices and — later, demonstrably — Indo-Aryan languages all moved through the greater Indus world, but at different times, at different scales, and by different mechanisms, and no one of those movements can be substituted for another. Direct, individual-level evidence (tooth-enamel isotopes, ancient DNA) proves *some* first-generation hinterland-to-city migration in the Mature Harappan period, *some* Indus-related individuals buried in Iran/Central Asia, and steppe-related ancestry entering northwestern South Asia only in the 2nd millennium BCE, after Mature Harappan urbanism. It does **not** prove mass replacement in either direction, it does not identify any Harappan language, and — with exactly one low-coverage genome from the Indus core (Rakhigarhi I6113) — it cannot characterise a civilisation of ~1 million km². Most long-distance Harappan contact (Meluhha–Mesopotamia, lapis, carnelian, shell, timber, cotton) is evidenced by goods, seals and foreign texts, which prove exchange and the presence of *some* travelling merchants/interpreters, not demographic migration. Farming in the northwest combines western domesticates (wheat, barley, sheep/goat) with local domestication/adoption (zebu, probably barley strains, cotton, later millets/rice systems); whether the western package arrived mainly with migrating farmers, with exchange, or in staggered episodes is still contested — and was reopened in 2025 by a radiocarbon redating of Mehrgarh that, if upheld, shortens its Neolithic by ~2,500 years. For language: the Indus signs are undeciphered, there is no bilingual, no agreed sign list (386–694 signs depending on author), and no methodologically accepted link from ancestry, place-names or substrate words to a single “Harappan language.” Multilingualism is a plausible *model*, not an observation. Dravidian, Para-Munda/other substrate, Elamo-Dravidian and Burushaski-isolate hypotheses all remain below proof; Elamo-Dravidian is contested/refuted in its strong form by mainstream historical linguists. Indo-Aryan arrival by migration in the 2nd millennium BCE is the consensus linguistic model and is *compatible* with — not proved by — the timing of steppe-related ancestry; the older “Aryan invasion destroyed Harappa” model is refuted.

**Confidence basis:** `verified live` for Valentine et al. 2015 (PLoS ONE, read in full text in the browser); `index` (search-result / publisher / repository text read) for all other primary papers. Where a claim rests on a secondary summary (Wikipedia synthesising Lukacs/Hemphill, Kennedy, Elfenbein, Witzel), it is flagged as such and downgraded.

**Negative closures treated as findings (AEE rule):** (1) No steppe ancestry in the available pre-2000 BCE Indus/Indus-Periphery genomes — a real, bounded negative, not “no contact ever.” (2) No archaeological horizon of violent invasion at 1900 BCE. (3) No accepted decipherment, and no accepted gene→language link. (4) No open, site-complete Indus gazetteer + inscription + isotope + aDNA dataset exists ready-made; integration is a construction task, not a download.

## 0. Epistemic governance — how this report is structured for spec-kit / AEE

### 0.1 Separation rule (applied throughout)

Every major proposition is decomposed into four layers. Mixing them is the single most common error in this literature.

| Layer | Definition here | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Observation (O)** | What a source directly reports measuring/finding, with n, context, date | “61 Rakhigarhi skeletal samples were screened; 1 yielded usable genome-wide data.” |
| **Inference (I)** | A conclusion that follows from observations + an explicit method, falsifiable if the method/data change | “I6113’s modelled ancestry contains no detectable steppe-pastoralist component under the qpAdm models used.” |
| **Model (M)** | A population/history scenario fitted to inferences (ANI/ASI, Indus Periphery Cline, demic diffusion) | “Indus Periphery individuals proxy the ancestry of un-sampled Indus-core people.” |
| **Interpretation (Interp.)** | A cultural/linguistic/historical meaning attached to a model | “Steppe-related migrants brought Indo-Aryan languages.” — interpretation of a genetic model + independent linguistics; genetics alone does not state it |

**Anti-teleology rule (owner direction):** A known later outcome (e.g. Indo-Aryan languages dominant in the north by the 1st millennium BCE; Dravidian languages dominant in the south) must **never** be used as a premise for reconstructing an earlier language/culture. Later distributions are *data to be explained*, with migration, shift, elite dominance, trade, and loss all as live rivals.

## 1. Movements directly supported — and by what evidence

**Direct = individual-level measurement on the person who moved (Class A) or direct measurement on the object/substance that moved (Class B).**

1. **Hinterland → city migration, Mature Harappan (DUR-001, supported, bounded).** Valentine et al. 2015, *PLoS ONE* — Sr/Pb in tooth enamel (childhood signature vs adult burial place) at Harappa (Cemetery R-37) and Farmana: the cemetery populations, accumulated over centuries, consist almost entirely of first-generation immigrants, with intra-individual shifts consistent with moving in childhood from geochemically distinct, resource-rich hinterlands. *Verified live:* https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/file?type=printable&id=10.1371/journal.pone.0123103 — The paper itself warns: total known Indus skeletal record ≈600 individuals; formal cemeteries are rare and selective (near-absence of infants, relatively good health/ornaments in R-37), so these are *a chosen subset*, not “the Harappans.” Earlier Sr/morphology work at Harappa and Lothal (summarised therein) likewise suggested residence change and urban–hinterland gene flow, with greater male variation at Harappa once read as matrilocality — a reading to treat as hypothesis, not fact.
2. **Indus-related people abroad (DUR-004, supported as ancestry outliers).** Narasimhan et al. 2019, *Science*: 11 individuals at Gonur Depe (BMAC, Turkmenistan) and Shahr-i Sokhta (Iran) are genetic outliers from their local populations, carrying a mixture of Iranian-related + AASI-related (Ancient Ancestral South Indian-related) ancestry with no steppe component — the “Indus Periphery” profile, matching the single Rakhigarhi core genome. Some were buried with South-Asian-associated objects. Inference: they, or their recent ancestors, came from the Indus sphere. It does not follow that they were “citizens,” spoke any particular language, or represent a diaspora community. Harvard summary: https://phys.org/news/2019-09-largest-ever-ancient-dna-illuminates-millennia-south.html
3. **Steppe-related ancestry arriving NW South Asia, 2nd millennium BCE (AFT-001, supported for sampled transect).** Same study: a time transect from Central Asia (Steppe MLBA groups moving south/east 2000–1500 BCE) into Swat (Late Bronze/Iron Age individuals, c. 1200–800 BCE cemeteries and related samples, all carrying some Steppe MLBA ancestry). Admixture dating (linkage/tract methods) places the Swat mixture process broadly in the early–mid 2nd millennium. Modern South Asians’ steppe component is modelled as primarily male-mediated and up to ~20% maximum in the groups studied — a *model estimate on modern + ancient data*, not a head-count of migrants.
4. **Local/regional mobility in the Neolithic–Chalcolithic (BEF-004, contested).** Dental biodistance at Mehrgarh (Lukacs; Lukacs & Hemphill): Neolithic vs Chalcolithic inhabitants differ dentally despite material continuity — consistent with moderate inflow; Neolithic Mehrgarh dentally closer to Chalcolithic Inamgaon (Deccan) than to its own Chalcolithic successors. Biodistance can be mimicked by drift/selection; there are still **no published Mehrgarh genomes** to test it.
5. **Diet-linked mobility / agro-pastoral movement (Class B, supported as practice, not head-count).** Archaeobotany + faunal + C/N isotopes where published: winter (rabi) wheat/barley systems in the west/northwest vs summer (kharif) millet/rice/tropical-pulse systems in Gujarat/Haryana villages (Masudpur I/VII by Rakhigarhi: rice, millets, pulses alongside wheat/barley, directly dated, before/during/after the urban floruit — Petrie/Bates). Faunal profiles and zebu genetics (BEF-001) show herds and a locally domesticated animal moving east/south over millennia. These prove *subsistence practices and animals moved*; seasonal pastoral transhumance is widely inferred from site location/fauna but rarely proved for a named individual — a gap isotopes on faunal teeth (sequential Sr/O) could close and mostly has not yet, for the Indus.
6. **Burial change as movement evidence — weak alone.** Cremation appearing in Cemetery H, fractional/cist burials in Swat, coffin burials at Harappa: burial rite is a *practice* that can spread with few people, be adopted, or be imposed. It upgrades to movement evidence **only** when paired with isotopes/aDNA on the same skeletons — which, for Cemetery H and PGW, is largely still missing. That missing pairing is the field’s biggest testable gap (§8, Q3).

**Rule (ledger):** an object’s provenance (Class B) licenses a claim about the *object’s* movement. A claim about *people* requires Class A evidence or a household/production/burial assemblage that exchange cannot plausibly produce. Most textbook “migrations” in the Indus literature are Class C patterns with Class A language — downgrade them on entry.

## 3. What can and cannot be inferred about language

**Can be inferred (with stated strength):**
- *Later* language geography and genealogy, from texts and the comparative method: Indo-Aryan is Indo-Iranian is Indo-European; its closest relatives are Iranian languages; Vedic Sanskrit’s oldest recoverable geography is the Greater Punjab/NW; Dravidian is an independent family with internal geography centred in central/south India plus the Brahui outlier and Kurukh/Malto in the east; Burushaski, Kusunda, Nihali etc. are isolates — proof that South Asia once held (and partly still holds) more linguistic diversity than the two big families explain.
- Contact, from loanword stratigraphy *when etymologies are regular*: Indo-Aryan absorbed non-Indo-European vocabulary in layers (LAN-003) — village life, local flora/fauna, agriculture terms are disproportionately non-Indo-European, implying prolonged bilingual contact with pre-existing NW populations, whoever they were. Dravidian and Indo-Aryan share areal features (retroflexion, quotative, gerund constructions) — a South Asian linguistic area built by multilingual contact over millennia. Retroflexion in particular is areal (also Burushaski, Munda, Hindu Kush languages — Witzel’s “retroflex belt”), so it cannot be assigned to one donor family.
- That Meluhha needed interpreters in Mesopotamia (Shu-ilishu seal/texts): i.e. at least one Indus language was *not* mutually intelligible with Akkadian/Sumerian/Elamite in the late 3rd millennium. That is nearly the only contemporary external observation about Harappan language(s), and it identifies none of them.

**Cannot be inferred:**
- From ancestry: no allele, haplogroup (incl. R1a), or qpAdm component is a language. Steppe ancestry + Indo-Aryan is a *correlation of two independently built models in time/space*, usable as consilience, never as identification (GEN-001).
- From geography alone: Brahui’s location (LAN-005), Dravidian place-name elements claimed for Maharashtra/Gujarat (Southworth), or river names — each is compatible with several histories (relic, later movement, substrate survival in names after language death). Place-names outlive languages routinely.
- From later texts as transcripts: the Rigveda (composed c. 1500–1200 BCE in mainstream dating, in the NW, after deurbanisation) is not a Harappan document; Sangam Tamil literature is ~2,000 years later still. Either may preserve older words/names — each such item needs its own etymological trial; neither can be read back wholesale (anti-teleology rule, §0.1).
- From sign statistics: conditional entropy / n-gram structure (Rao et al.) is compatible with linguistic encoding and, per the Farmer–Sproat–Witzel critique, cannot by itself exclude non-linguistic systems (LAN-002). No statistical score is a decipherment, and **no PRED/decipherment score may be derived from mobility/context evidence** (§9).
- A single “Harappan language”: with inscriptions averaging ~5 signs, no bilingual, unstable sign lists, and possible regional sign variation (Mahadevan’s own tables break the corpus by site/object/direction), singularity is an assumption. LAN-007 (multilingualism) is the safer *design prior* and remains not-yet-testable as a finding.

## 5. Ancient DNA — critical assessment

**The core problem is arithmetic.** For the entire Indus core, published genome-wide data = **one individual** (Shinde et al. 2019, *Cell*, I6113, Rakhigarhi, context-dated c. 2800–2300 BCE, female). Source facts (EXACT): 61 skeletal samples screened; 109 libraries built for the best sample (68 retained after filtering a low-coverage extraction); tens of thousands of usable SNPs (~31,760 authentic variants in the *Cell* commentary’s summary; 86,440 SNPs covered ≥1× in the paper’s merged data — the discrepancy itself illustrates filter-dependence). Collagen was too poor for direct AMS dating of I6113; the date is archaeological context + habitation charcoal dates. Interpretive facts (MODEL): no detectable steppe or post-6000 BCE Iranian-farmer ancestry; Iranian-related component best modelled as a lineage that diverged from Iranian farmers’ ancestors *before* they admixed with Anatolian farmers; remainder AASI-related. Sources: https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674(19)30967-5 and commentary https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674(19)31078-5

**Limitations, in ledger order:**
1. **n=1, one site, eastern Indus.** Rakhigarhi cannot stand for Sindh, Gujarat, Baluchistan or the Makran. Authors and commentators state this; secondary/political uses often omit it. Patterson’s contemporary remark that the IVC was likely “genetically extremely diverse” is the correct prior.
2. **Low coverage / pseudo-haploid calling.** Genotypes are largely single-read calls; downstream ancestry proportions carry wide, model-dependent uncertainty, and absence-of-ancestry claims are bounded by detection power. A non-peer-reviewed critique alleging reference-bias inflation in I6113 circulates online (https://eurasiandna.com/an-ancient-harappan-genome-lacks-ancestry-from-steppe-pastoralists-or-iranian-farmers-a-critique/) — flag: *not peer-reviewed, do not weight equally with Cell/Science*; the legitimate, weaker point (low coverage limits confidence) is already in the primary literature.
3. **Preservation/cremation bias.** Hot, fluctuating lowland climates degrade DNA; the ~600 known Indus skeletons are themselves a selected, mostly unburned minority of the dead. The genomic record is therefore biased toward whoever received inhumation in excavated cemeteries — the same selection that affects DUR-001. Mohenjo-daro, the largest city, contributes **zero** genomes.
4. **Indus Periphery is a proxy, not a sample (DUR-004 dependency).** The cline is defined from outliers *outside* the Indus. Using it to reconstruct “the IVC genome,” then citing its match to I6113 as confirmation, risks circularity: I6113 (n=1) is the only independent core check.
5. **Swat is not the Punjab plain (AFT-001 dependency).** The post-Indus transect is strong for the Swat/Inner Asian Mountain Corridor route and silent for Gujarat, Sindh, the doab and the Deccan in 1900–1000 BCE. Admixture *dates* are model outputs (tract-length assumptions, generation time, single- vs multi-pulse); Narasimhan et al.’s own framing is a process across 2000–1500 BCE, not an event.
6. **Modern-DNA back-projection (Moorjani et al. 2013).** ANI–ASI mixture dated c. 4,200–1,900 years ago from linkage disequilibrium in 73 modern groups (DOI 10.1016/j.ajhg.2013.07.006; https://repository.ias.ac.in/107395/) — ingenious, but ANI/ASI are statistical constructs fitted to modern people; the 2019 ancient data replaced them with a three-source cline model (AASI / Indus Periphery / Steppe MLBA). Treat ANI/ASI numbers as superseded model history, not measurements.
7. **Sex-bias and haplogroup shorthand.** “Steppe ancestry is male-biased” is a model comparison (autosomes vs X, modern + ancient); single haplogroups (R1a-Z93 etc.) track one paternal line among thousands of ancestors and must never headline a population claim — press/political coverage repeatedly violated this for Rakhigarhi (both “R1a absent, invasion disproved” and the inverse).

**What the aDNA *does* establish, robustly:** (i) a distinctive Iranian-related + AASI-related ancestry profile existed in the Indus sphere by the Mature Harappan period without steppe input; (ii) that profile is the largest single contributor to later South Asian ancestry in the models; (iii) steppe MLBA ancestry enters the sampled NW record after, not before, the Mature Harappan floruit; (iv) post-Indus South Asia formed by mixture of Indus-related groups with northern (steppe-carrying) and peninsular (AASI-rich) groups — ANI/ASI formation in the 2nd millennium in the 2019 model. All four are about *ancestry*, none is about language, ethnicity, or culture as lived.

## 8. Falsifiable, pre-registrable study questions

Each question names variables, null/comparison, required evidence, falsifier, and the ecological-fallacy / linkage block where one exists. **General block (applies to Q1–Q2, the inscription questions):** inscription-bearing objects and isotope/aDNA individuals are almost never the *same* objects/people, and most inscribed objects lack sealed, dated contexts. Any site-level correlation is therefore an **ecological (aggregate) association**, not evidence that mobile people carried particular signs. A valid test must (a) state this, (b) use site-period as the unit, (c) show the effect survives the listed controls, and (d) never upgrade a surviving correlation to a claim about individuals, languages or decipherment.

**Q1 — Does sign-repertoire variation track independently measured mobility/exchange, net of confounders?**
Variables: DV = pairwise site-period sign-repertoire distance (e.g. Bray–Curtis on sign frequencies, M77 numbering, pre-registered corpus rules incl./excl. damaged & multi-line texts). IVs = (i) exchange-network distance (shared provenanced materials, D8; shared ceramic chaînes), (ii) mobility index (proportion non-local Sr/Pb individuals per site-period, D3 — available for ≤3 sites, so this arm may be *not-yet-testable* and must be reported as such). Controls: chronological distance (D4 dates), geographic distance, object-type mix (seal vs tablet vs pottery graffiti), corpus size per site, excavation/reporting intensity (seasons, publication language, museum collection history).
Null: partial Mantel / MRM — sign distance is explained by geography + chronology + object type + sampling alone; exchange/mobility add nothing.
Falsifier/weaken: exchange/mobility coefficients ≈0 or wrong-signed under the null model, or effect disappears when Mohenjo-daro/Harappa (corpus-dominant sites) are jackknifed out → claim weakened to “corpus artefact.”

**Q2 — Is regional sign variation real at all, or a preservation/object-type artefact?**
Variables: sign richness/evenness per site-period, rarefied to common sample size; object type; medium; context type (workshop vs burial vs surface).
Null: after rarefaction + object-type stratification, site explains no more variance than a permutation of site labels.
Falsifier of “regional dialects of signs”: null not rejected → all downstream Q1-style work must stop (negative closure). Falsifier of the null: site effect persists within a single object type (e.g. steatite seals only) across periods.

**Q3 — Did Cemetery H / early PGW transitions involve incoming people?**
Variables: Sr/O (+aDNA if recoverable) on Cemetery H and earliest PGW-associated human remains vs Mature Harappan baselines from the same catchments; local baselines from fauna/water.
Null: isotopic/ancestry distributions indistinguishable from local Mature Harappan range (continuity model, cf. Kennedy biodistance).
Falsifier of migration form: null holds with adequate n (pre-register n and power: e.g. ≥30 individuals per phase per catchment) → IND-AFT-003 migration reading weakened. Falsifier of continuity: a dated subset is systematically non-local / carries new ancestry → ritual-change-only model weakened. *Block:* cremated remains limit aDNA (Sr survives cremation better — IsoArcH cremation work is methodologically relevant); if preservation yields n below the pre-registered threshold, verdict = not-yet-testable, not “continuity proved.”

**Q4 — Was the Late Harappan eastward shift a population movement or in-place crop adaptation?**
Variables: per-site archaeobotanical spectra + direct crop 14C dates (D9/D4); human C/N isotopes (millet/rice C4/C3 signal) where bone survives; settlement foundation/abandonment dates.
Null (adaptation): eastern sites show gradual in-place addition of kharif crops with isotopic continuity of people. Rival (movement): abrupt full-package replacement + non-local human isotope signatures at foundation.
Falsifier: whichever pattern fails to appear at pre-registered effect size in ≥3 independent site clusters. *Block:* bone preservation in the doab is poor — pre-register the fallback (faunal isotopes + settlement chronometry) and its weaker verdict ceiling.

**Q5 — Demic vs cultural diffusion of farming at the Mehrgarh transition (test of BEF-002/BEF-003b).**
Variables: new enamel/short-lived-sample 14C series from sealed Neolithic contexts; (aspirational) Neolithic aDNA; western-domesticate first-appearance dates along an Iran→Baluchistan transect of sites.
Null A (demic): first farming dates form a west→east time gradient consistent with population spread, with no earlier local farming. Null B (local/independent): farming traits appear without gradient / predate western comparanda.
Falsifiers: replicated dates showing Period I ≪5000 BCE refute Mutin et al.’s revision and weaken late-diffusion; replicated c. 5200–4900 BCE start + a clean west→east gradient weaken independent-invention. *Current status:* single-study revision — Q5 is the replication study itself.

**Q6 — Was Mature Harappan standardisation simultaneous (administered) or wave-like (network adoption)?**
Variables: first-appearance dates (Bayesian, D4) per site for: standard weights, brick ratio, seal+sign use, drainage/planned layout. DV = spread in first-appearance dates vs distance from putative cores.
Null: dates are simultaneous within dating error across regions (administered horizon). Rival: significant distance-decay (adoption wave) or region-first patterns (polycentric).
Falsifier: the losing pattern’s posterior support < pre-registered threshold. *Value:* distinguishes political-integration interpretations from network ones without invoking migration at all.

**Q7 — Do provenanced-material networks predict craft-style networks better than geography does?**
Variables: site×site matrices — (a) shared geological sources (D8), (b) ceramic petrography/chaîne similarity, (c) bead-drilling technique similarity; geographic + river-network distance as controls (MRM/partial Mantel as Q1).
Null: geography/river distance explains all shared variance.
Falsifier: source-sharing adds no predictive power → “craft networks followed materials” weakened; conversely, if technique similarity tracks *source* rather than *distance*, pure-diffusion-by-proximity is weakened. No people-movement claim is licensed by either outcome (objects/techniques ≠ people, §2 rule).

**Q8 — Substrate stratigraphy test (linguistic, no genetics).**
Variables: Rigvedic words with proposed non-Indo-European etymologies, coded blind by ≥2 etymologists for: earliest textual layer, semantic field, proposed donor (Dravidian / Para-Munda-type / other / Indo-European after all). Inter-rater agreement pre-registered (κ threshold).
Null (LAN-003 simple form): earliest-layer loans are predominantly Dravidian by regular correspondences.
Falsifier: earliest layer is predominantly non-Dravidian or unassignable at the κ threshold → simple Dravidian-first model weakened (Witzel model strengthened); the converse distribution weakens Witzel’s sequence. *Block:* etymology is judgement-laden — if κ is low, the honest verdict is “substrate stratigraphy is not currently a discriminating instrument,” a valid negative closure.

**Q9 — Brahui discriminator (LAN-005).**
Variables: loanword stratigraphy inside Brahui (Balochi/Iranian layers vs inherited North Dravidian core, incl. kinship/agricultural vocabulary), Brahui–Kurukh/Malto shared innovations, and any dated attestations/toponymy in Baluchistan.
Comparison: relic model predicts deep, pre-Balochi contact layers + archaic retentions lost elsewhere in Dravidian; late-arrival model predicts a shallower, more southern/central Dravidian profile at arrival.
Falsifier: a stratigraphy incompatible with one model at pre-registered criteria. *Block:* with no pre-modern Brahui texts (earliest literature c. 18th c.), the test may return not-yet-testable — state this in the pre-registration, do not substitute genetics (Brahui speakers’ genomes reflect Baluchistan admixture history, not their language’s arrival date).

## 9. What a responsible study design must NOT claim

1. **No gene→language identification.** No ancestry component, haplogroup, or admixture date names a language. (GEN-001 is logged *refuted as worded* precisely to block this move in the ledger.)
2. **No ethnic labels projected backward.** “Aryan,” “Dravidian” (as a people/race), “Harappan ethnicity,” caste, or any modern ethnonym may not be attached to genomes, skeletons, pots or seals. Linguistic family names may be used only for languages, with dates.
3. **No object→mass-migration inference.** A seal in Ur, lapis in Mohenjo-daro, or a BMAC pot in Baluchistan licenses a claim about exchange/contact, scaled as in §2 — never, alone, about population movement, and never about *mass* movement.
4. **No later text as Harappan transcript.** Rigveda, Avestan, Sangam, Mesopotamian texts (beyond what they say about *their own* trade world, e.g. Meluhha goods/interpreters) are not evidence of what Harappans said, believed or called themselves.
5. **No PRED / decipherment scoring from contextual mobility evidence.** Isotope, aDNA, provenance or network results may not be entered as features, priors or validation for a claimed reading of Indus signs, and no “language-family probability” for the script may be output from such data. Sign-internal work must stand or fall on sign-internal + bilingual evidence (which does not currently exist).
6. **No absence-of-evidence upgrades.** “No steppe ancestry in n=1 + 11” ≠ “no steppe contact anywhere”; “no destruction layer” ≠ “no conflict ever”; “no genomes from Mohenjo-daro” ≠ any statement about Mohenjo-daro’s people.
7. **No package deals.** Crops, animals, metals, burial rites, genes and words each get their own claim, date and falsifier. A “farming-language-people package” entry is invalid in the ledger.
8. **No teleology.** Later language maps (D10/D11) are outcomes to explain, never premises (§0.1). Any analysis joining them to Bronze Age sites must include an explicit temporal model and a shift/loss rival.
9. **No silent model-shopping.** qpAdm/ADMIXTURE/admixture-date outputs must be reported with the source populations tried and rejected, sign-list version (for script work), corpus exclusion rules, and gazetteer version — the spec-kit “dependency” field is mandatory, not metadata garnish.

## 10. Strongest defensible synthesis — in plain English

**Before the cities (to c. 3300 BCE).** *People:* small-scale movement along the Bolan and other passes, and along the Iranian-plateau/Baluchistan interface, is very likely over millennia — biodistance hints at it, genomes to prove its size do not exist. *Genes:* we have no direct readings from this period in the northwest at all; everything said about it is back-projection. *Goods:* from the start, far-travelled things — marine shell, lapis, turquoise — reach Mehrgarh, so exchange networks precede farming villages’ growth. *Technologies/practices:* a mixed farming toolkit arrives/takes shape — western wheat, barley, sheep and goats alongside cattle that become the locally domesticated zebu, and local crafts (including very early dentistry) that owe nothing obvious to the west. Whether farmers themselves carried the western toolkit in, or local people adopted it piecemeal, is unresolved — and a 2025 redating that makes Mehrgarh ~2,500 years younger has, if correct, tilted the argument toward later diffusion from the west without settling it. *Words/languages:* nothing whatsoever is known.

**During the cities (3300–1900 BCE, urban peak 2600–1900).** *People:* provably mobile at the individual scale — many of the formally buried at Harappa and Farmana grew up somewhere else and moved as children/young people; some Indus-connected people ended their lives buried in Iran and Turkmenistan; merchants and at least one interpreter worked between the Indus and Mesopotamia. *Genes:* the sampled Indus-sphere profile — a distinctive older Iranian-related lineage mixed long before with South Asia’s indigenous hunter-gatherer-related ancestry — contains no steppe input; it later becomes the biggest ingredient in South Asians generally. *Goods:* this is the great age of moved things: lapis, carnelian, shell, timber, cotton, ivory and finished seals travelling to the Gulf and Mesopotamia; copper, tin and Gulf goods travelling back; raw stone routinely moved 300–800 km to workshops. *Technologies/practices:* standardised weights, bricks, seals, drainage and craft recipes are shared across an enormous area — shared *know-how and rules*, spread by networks and specialists, with no need for a single state or a single people to explain them; villages near the cities already farm both winter and summer crops in a flexible mix. *Words/languages:* at least one Indus language was foreign enough to need interpreters in Akkad; how many languages the cities held, and which families they belonged to, is unknown. Anyone who tells you the Harappans “spoke Dravidian” (or Sanskrit, or Elamite) as a fact is selling you a hypothesis as a finding.

**After the cities (1900–1000 BCE and after).** *People/settlement:* city people do not vanish — urban life unwinds, and settlement shifts east and south into Haryana, western UP and Gujarat, with smaller, more rural communities; there is no invasion horizon and no massacre layer. Climate weakening (monsoon) and river changes press on the system, but how much weight each cause carries is still argued. *Genes:* in the northwest, a genuinely new ingredient — steppe-pastoralist-related ancestry coming through the Central Asian mountain corridor — appears and mixes with Indus-related populations during the 2nd millennium, in the Swat record measurably by the late 2nd/early 1st millennium; mixtures of Indus-related, steppe-carrying and peninsular groups in this era create the ancestry gradients South Asia still shows. We still have no genomes from the Late Harappan heartlands themselves in this period — the most important missing data in the whole story. *Practices:* cremation spreads in the Punjab (Cemetery H), new grave forms appear in Swat, rice and millets gain ground in the east, iron and the horse become ordinary in the 1st millennium. Each of these *could* travel with people, with fashion, or with both — burial or pottery change alone cannot tell us which. *Words/languages:* here, at last, language history becomes visible on its own evidence: Indo-Aryan — a branch of the same family as Persian and, distantly, Greek and English — is being composed in the northwest (the Rigveda), already full of borrowed local words for village life and local plants and animals, first from one or more lost northern languages (not, in the earliest layer, mainly from Dravidian, on Witzel’s influential reading) and only later clearly from Dravidian as Vedic culture moves east and south. The likeliest reading, held together by linguistics and compatible with the genetic timing but *proved by neither alone*, is that Indo-Aryan arrived with the 2nd-millennium migrants and spread mostly by people adopting it, intermarrying and shifting language over centuries — not by invasion, and not in a day. Dravidian languages, meanwhile, are certainly old in the peninsula and centre, Brahui sits isolated in Baluchistan as a genuine unsolved puzzle (ancient relic or later arrival — both still stand), Burushaski and other isolates preserve fragments of a once much more varied linguistic map, and Elamite, across the Gulf, stays an isolate: the grand Elamo-Dravidian bridge has not held its builders’ weight.

**What remains unknown, honestly:** the language(s) of the Indus cities; the size and organisation of every migration named above; the genomes of Mohenjo-daro, of Neolithic Mehrgarh, and of Late Harappan Punjab/Gujarat; why standardisation held and why it let go; and whether the sign system’s regional variation encodes dialect, administration, or merely workshops. Those unknowns are not embarrassments — in a falsifier-first program they are the pre-registration list (§8).
