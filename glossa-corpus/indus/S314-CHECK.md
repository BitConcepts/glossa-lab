# S314-CHECK — the §3.14 "M342/M176 dominant" sentence, verified against its own record (2026-11-18)

**Scope.** One sentence only, per the commission. Nothing was edited.
The sentence, in `pierson_2026_indus_decipherment_v11_corrected.tex`,
§3.14 "Formula Semantic Clustering" (line ~657):

> "All domains share the same terminal sign profile (M342/M176
> dominant) — a measured fact about the model's own sign assignments,
> consistent with a common positional skeleton across the model's
> domains."

**Verdict: WRONG EVEN THERE.** Under its own record's own measure,
M342/M176 are not dominant in any domain. Detail below; the exact
corrective sentence is at the end.

## (b) What "domains" and "terminal sign profile" mean in the record

- **Domains.** The 97 INITIAL-sign clusters (the distinct H+M initial
  signs) are grouped by the semantic domain this model assigns to each
  cluster's proposed reading: UNRESOLVED (51 clusters, 845 seals),
  ANIMAL_GUILD (25, 446), DEITY_TITLE (9, 135), MATERIAL (6, 145),
  CIVIC_ROLE (6, 99). A seal belongs to the domain of its first sign.
- **Terminal sign profile.** The script (lines 241–249) counts, for
  every seal of length ≥ 2 whose first sign is an H+M sign, the seal's
  **last sign**, accumulated per domain (`domain_terminals`). The
  record's `domain_terminal_profiles` field stores, per domain, the
  five most common terminal signs with their seal counts
  (`most_common(5)`). That is the only per-domain terminal measure the
  record computes, and it is the measure the paper's phrase "terminal
  sign profile" refers to (the section itself says the per-cluster
  list carries "each cluster's seals and terminal profile").

## Where the sentence came from

The record's `key_findings` contains the line "Cross-domain pattern:
all domains share terminal signs (M342/M176) — same grammar skeleton."
In the generating script that line is a **hardcoded string**, not a
computed result, and the profiles computed and stored beside it do not
support the "(M342/M176)" specificity. The paper's sentence follows
that gloss and strengthens it — "share terminal signs" became "share
the same terminal sign profile (M342/M176 dominant)". What the record
does support is the weaker observation underneath: the domains draw
on an overlapping pool of terminal signs (M233, M367, M051, M305,
M099 and M065 each appear in more than one domain's top five).

## (c) Verdict and corrective sentence

**WRONG EVEN THERE.** No quantity in the §3.14 record — not the
per-domain terminal profiles, not the per-cluster top terminals, and
(not its measure, but confirming) not the corpus-wide seal-ending
counts — makes M342/M176 dominant. The record's actual result is: a
shared terminal pool with a different leading sign in each domain.

**Exact corrective sentence** (record-supported; replaces the flagged
sentence and nothing else):

> "The domains draw on a shared pool of terminal signs, but no pair
> of signs dominates them: the most frequent terminal sign differs by
> domain (M367 in UNRESOLVED, M233 in ANIMAL_GUILD, M051 in MATERIAL,
> M065 in DEITY_TITLE, M099 in CIVIC_ROLE), and neither M342 nor M176
> heads any domain's terminal profile — a measured fact about the
> model's own sign assignments, consistent with a common positional
> skeleton across the model's domains."

Nothing in this check is unsettled: the record is complete for the
question asked (its profiles are top-5 lists, so a claim about signs
outside every top five — e.g. M176's exact per-domain counts — cannot
be stated from it; the corrective sentence claims nothing of that
kind).
