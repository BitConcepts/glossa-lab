This amendment governs the draft `2026-10-26-spec-draft-step0g-implantation-instrument.md` (SHA-256 `53b4ae9e…`), which is **not edited**: the draft and the halt record stay byte-untouched as history, and the amended design is the draft as modified by this file. Where the two conflict, this amendment governs — within its scope, and only within its scope.

## 1. The true solo pool (the fact the halt established)

The draft's feasibility arithmetic inherited the G44-S2 skeleton's proportions, in which ONLY made up ≈ 0.0209 of slots — "on the order of 244 solo slots" in the frame, ≈ 122 per compilation. The pinned frame's recounted role totals (halt record §2, verified by an independent recount in the halted run) are FIRST 2,942 / MIDDLE 5,818 / LAST 2,942 / **ONLY 103** (share 0.0087). The frame holds **103 solo inscriptions, not ~244**; the first run's split dealt 50 to the fitting compilation and 53 to the scoring compilation. Every ONLY number below is derived from 103 and from the per-compilation solo counts of whatever split a run actually draws, never from the skeleton's shares again.

## 2. The ONLY family, re-derived (§§4.7, 5 of the draft, amended)

The halt record (§4) named the constraints any repair must satisfy simultaneously: the §4.7 family cap at 25% of solo slots is ≈ 13 cells per compilation; one ONLY-planted sign at the draft's floors needs ≈ 11–16 cells by itself; spillover, drawn totals, and the sign cap compete for the same cells. The amended rules, each a lever the halt record named:

1. **Draw weight unchanged (0.04); sign cap 3 → 1.** The cap leaves room for at most one minimum-size ONLY-planted sign per compilation (halt record §2); the amended cap is therefore **one** ONLY-planted sign per ledger. Excess ONLY draws are reassigned by the draft's existing deterministic rule (fewest-assigned of FIRST/MIDDLE/LAST, ties in that order), recorded in the ledger. Draws with zero ONLY-planted signs remain possible (≈ 17% of draws at weight 0.04); such a ledger simply plants no ONLY-position sign, and the family ONLY total is zero.
2. **Quota band:** an ONLY-planted sign now draws from the common amended band **[12, 18]** (§4 of this amendment), replacing [20, 28].
3. **Concentration range unchanged as drawn** (uniform(0.55, 0.70)) — but the planted cell is set by a **named reduction operation**, the operation the draft lacked: per compilation, **cell = min(round-half-up(a × quota), C_max)**, where **C_max = floor(min(30, 25% of that compilation's solo slots))**. The ledger records the drawn *a*, the uncapped cell, the capped cell, and the **effective concentration a_eff = cell / quota**, which is the planted parameter of record.
4. **Spillover is zero.** A non-ONLY-planted sign's ONLY cell is exactly 0; its remaining tokens distribute over the two remaining non-ONLY classes in proportion to the compilation's base shares, by the draft's largest-remainder rule. (Draft §4.4 distributed over three classes; the ≈ 0.9%-of-mass ONLY dribble from 43 signs was unpriced demand against a 13-cell cap.)
5. **Feasibility precondition:** with an ONLY-planted sign in the ledger, **C_max ≥ 10 in both compilations** (equivalently, ≥ 40 solo inscriptions each) is required. It is checked on the drawn split at ledger construction, before any implantation; a split violating it files the draft §4.3 design-record event (halt and report), exactly as the first run halted. At 103 solos dealt alternately, each compilation expects ≈ 51–52; 40 is more than two standard deviations low (hypergeometric sd ≈ 5), so the precondition binds on roughly 1% of splits.
6. **Expected profile q(s)** is defined from the ledger cells (q[c] = cells[c] / quota, per compilation) for every designated sign — one rule, no divergence between "expected" and planted. The scorer never reads q(s); this is bookkeeping only.

**Worst-case proof (not expected case).** With at most one ONLY-planted sign and spillover zero, the family ONLY total in each compilation is that sign's single capped cell, hence ≤ C_max ≤ min(30, 25% of solo slots) — the §4.7 cap holds **by construction, for every draw and every split**. The planted class remains the realized argmax (draft R1's derived print): the other classes' largest possible cell is bounded by the MIDDLE share of the remainder (≤ 0.4972 × (quota − cell) + 1 by the largest-remainder rule), and cell ≥ 10 with quota ≤ 18 keeps cell strictly greatest in all cases; in the uncapped case cell = round-half-up(a × quota) ≥ round-half-up(0.55 × 12) = 7 against a remainder of ≤ 5 tokens split three ways. When the cap binds (C_max ≥ 10, quota ≤ 18), a_eff = C_max / quota ≥ 10/18 ≈ 0.556 — the capped sign's realized concentration stays at or above the draft's 0.55 floor. These claims were additionally verified numerically over the full discrete family: 74 base-share vectors around the pinned frame's × all quotas 12–18 × all concentrations in their ranges × all C_max 10–30 — **222,222 cell constructions, zero violations** (`worst_case_sweep.py`, amendment scratch).

## 3. What this does not decide

The ONLY-position coverage of the amended instrument is at most one sign, and that sign's concentration may be capped to a_eff. That is the honest price of a 103-inscription solo pool, recorded here and in §8 as a limit — the same kind of limit the draft's §11 already carried for the old narrowing, now with the arithmetic shown.

## 4. The capacity repair (a second parameter-family defect, found by the Phase-1 census computation — flagged, not hidden)

Computing the §5 census required executing the fitting implantation on the halted run's published inputs. It failed at a cell — and the failure is a defect in the draft's parameter family of the same kind as the ONLY defect, in the **quota/budget/per-source-cap** system rather than in ONLY:

- The draft's per-source-sign cap (§4.5: a real sign loses at most min(30 tokens, 20% of its tokens in the compilation)) funds an aggregate of **893 tokens (fitting) / 865 (scoring)** on the first run's split — because the 20% is taken per sign with a floor, and the 30-token cap binds the few large signs that hold most tokens.
- The draft's quota band [20, 40] with its 20% budget demands **1,209 / 1,151** tokens — roughly 320 more than the caps fund in aggregate, and more than the FIRST-class capacity in particular (cap-usable FIRST tokens 351 / 348 vs family FIRST demand 374 / 351 on the halted ledger). The draft's §4.3 margins checked demand against slot pools (≈ 1,400 FIRST slots) but never against the per-source cap that §4.5 imposes on those slots.
- **Repair (parameter family only; both integrity rules themselves unchanged):** the quota band is amended to **uniform integer [12, 18] for every designated sign**, and the §4.3 budget-scaling floor falls with it, **18 → 12**. The 20% budget rule is untouched and becomes inert (the largest possible drawn sum is 44 × 18 = 792, below any plausible budget ≈ 1,150); it remains as the R3 ceiling. The per-source cap is untouched.
- **Feasibility verification is extended accordingly:** at ledger construction, the run verifies and prints — per compilation — aggregate demand vs aggregate cap capacity, per-class demand vs per-class cap-usable capacity, and per-class demand vs slot pools, alongside the draft's §4.3 margins. Any failure is the draft §4.3 design-record event (halt before implantation, report), the course the first run took.
- **Design-stage verification:** on the first run's split, the amended family draws quota sums 665 / 658 against capacities 893 / 865, with the tightest per-class ratio at 47% of capacity (FIRST, fitting: demand 165 vs 351) — and the full pipeline (both implantations + independent recount) executes end to end on design-stage draws (amendment scratch, `rehearsal_design_stage.py`). The cost is stated, not buried: at 12–18 attestations per sign, per-sign resolution is coarser than the draft's §4.3 rationale assumed (position shares move in steps of ≈ 0.06–0.08 per token); position realization is exact, so R1 is unaffected, and the neighbor component averages continuous evidence over ≈ 11–17 neighbor-bearing attestations.

## 5. The neighbor side: census on the record, and the amended draw (§§4.7, 5, amended)

**The census the halt never reached, computed.** Basis: the first run's published split; a design-stage ledger drawn under the amended rules' predecessors with neighbor classes over all four classes (design seed 20271001, published here); fitting implantation per draft §4.6; final fitting profiles; the §4.7 test applied to the unimplanted scoring compilation (5,615 neighbor-bearing attestations of 5,756). Pool sizes P(c) — attestations whose neighbor evidence for c is at least 0.55:

| Neighbor class | P(c) | Eligible (≥ 300)? |
|---|---|---|
| FIRST | 706 | yes |
| MIDDLE | 2,900 | yes |
| LAST | 513 | yes |
| **ONLY** | **0** | **no** |

P(ONLY) is not near the threshold; it is **zero** (the largest ONLY neighbor evidence anywhere in the scoring compilation is 0.3333). The reason is structural: neighbor evidence for ONLY is a mean of neighbors' solo shares, the frame's solo share is 0.0087, and no real sign's profile concentrates in ONLY enough to lift a two-neighbor mean to 0.55. No split or seed variation available to a rerun can plausibly move a structural zero to 300; the rerun recomputes the census in its own order and the draft's rule governs there, but the design consequence is taken now, on this record:

1. **Neighbor classes are drawn uniform over the eligible set {FIRST, MIDDLE, LAST}.** No designated sign is planted to ONLY as a neighbor class. The instrument therefore **does not test ONLY-neighbor discrimination** — draft §11's conditional limit ("if the census finds the ONLY neighbor pool ineligible…") is now actual, and is carried into §8.
2. **Neighbor-class cap 12.** At most 12 designated signs per neighbor class; excess draws in label order are reassigned to MIDDLE (the eligible class with the largest pool), recorded in the ledger. This relieves the measured thin-tail competition documented in §6 (under the old uniform draw, 17 of the halted run's 44 signs drew ONLY as neighbor class, and FIRST/LAST draws compete for a few hundred high-evidence tokens).
3. **Mass ranges sized to the measured supply:** target neighbor mass m(s) is drawn per final neighbor class — **FIRST and LAST: uniform(0.50, 0.68); MIDDLE: uniform(0.55, 0.80)** — replacing uniform(0.55, 0.85) for all. The sizing evidence is §6; the supply tables (evidence distribution by class and slot class) are in the amendment scratch (`diagnostic_pools.py` output).
4. **Fallback retained.** If a rerun's in-run census ever disagreed with this record on any class's eligibility, the draft's §4.7 reassignment rule applies and is recorded. One clarification, with its reason stated: fitting implantation precedes ledger finalization in the draft's own §4.6 order, so it ranks toward drawn classes; for any sign whose neighbor class were reassigned by such a disagreement, the fitting-side R2 quantity is evaluated against its **drawn** class (the class fitting implantation actually targeted), while the scoring side is evaluated against the final class at 0.08 exactly as §6 specifies. No scoring-side check is weakened by this clarification.

## 6. The R2 feasibility finding (read before any rerun is dispatched)

Phase 1's computations established one further fact about the amended design, and this amendment records it at full strength rather than letting a rerun discover it expensively: **the §6 gate's R2 neighbor-realization tolerances (per-sign 0.08 scoring / 0.12 fitting; family mean ± 0.03) are not achievable on this background under the draft's §4.5 selection algorithm, at any parameter setting this amendment is empowered to choose.**

**Mechanism (measured, not conjectured).** A replay diagnostic on the design-stage rehearsal decomposes each sign's realized-minus-planted neighbor error into (H1) selection-time shortfall — the mean evidence of the tokens selection actually picked, measured with the ranking inputs — mean **−0.013**, only 2 of 44 signs beyond 0.08; and (H2) **post-selection drag** — the same tokens' evidence in the final stream — mean **−0.031**, one-sided, with per-sign tails to **−0.18**. H2's cause: selection prefers tokens whose neighbors give high evidence for the planted class; when a later sign's implantation relabels such a neighbor to a designated sign, that neighbor's profile becomes an unrelated planted profile, and the evidence regresses toward the designated family's mean for that coordinate (≈ 0.29). Roughly a fifth of implanted tokens have a neighbor relabeled after their selection. The drag grows with the planted mass (higher targets select higher evidence, which has further to fall), and the fitting side adds the draft's known provisional-profile shift on top. Separately, the evidence supply for FIRST and LAST is thin and bimodal at high values (of 5,615 neighbor-bearing attestations, those with evidence ≥ 0.75 number in the low hundreds for LAST), so high masses also compete for a scarce tail (H1's tail cases: −0.128, −0.110 on the rehearsal draw).

**Sizing evidence (gate only — no legs were computed in any sizing run; verdict-fitting was neither done nor possible from this output).** 168 design-stage ledger draws on the published split, across seven configurations, each implanted in both compilations and audited by the independent recount: **zero gate passes.**

The §5 configuration is adopted because it is the closest to the contract on every summary — family means at or inside the ±0.03 boundary in several draws, argmax misses within the ≤ 4 allowance in shown draws, the only fitting-side passes — not because it achieves the contract. On the final amended rules, the design-stage draw (seed 20271001) passes R1, R3, R4 and R2's family-mean and argmax-miss conditions, and fails R2 on per-sign tails alone: **0.154 scoring (tolerance 0.08), 0.128 fitting (tolerance 0.12)**.

**What was not done.** No bar, tolerance, or certification condition was adjusted to meet the design (§6's tolerances are bars; this amendment's scope forbids it). The §4.5 selection algorithm was not altered (it is the method, not a parameter; replacing it is a new design act). No leg was computed during sizing.

**Consequence, stated plainly.** A rerun under this amendment is expected — on the evidence above — to implant successfully, pass R1/R3/R4, and **fail the gate at R2**, filing **INCOMPLETE — IMPLANTATION DEFECT**, which counts toward kill criterion KI-1 (two successive gate failures close the implantation approach). The cures for the R2 shortfall lie outside the parameter family: a selection-algorithm redesign (e.g., selection that anticipates post-implantation neighborhoods), or a gate-tolerance redesign — each a fresh owner-level design act, neither smuggled into this amendment. Whether to spend a KI-1 strike on the rerun as amended, hold the line here, or commission one of those design acts is the owner's decision, taken with this section in hand.

## 8. Limits the amended design carries (updating draft §11 for the amended family)

- ONLY position classes are planted on at most one sign, possibly at a capped effective concentration, possibly on none; ONLY-neighbor discrimination is not tested at all. A validation pass under this amendment says correspondingly less about ONLY-heavy batches — by arithmetic necessity, on the record.
- Per-sign resolution is coarser at quotas of 12–18 (the capacity repair's cost, §4).
- The gate is measured to fail at R2 on typical draws (§6); a pass, if a favorable draw achieves one, remains a pass under §7.5 — the bars are the bars — but §6 is the standing expectation against which any single run's outcome should be read.
- Background exposure is as the draft's §3.3 stated; this amendment adds that the halted run's split and ledger are public on the record, the design-stage computations above reused that published split with the design seeds stated, and any rerun uses **fresh seeds (model, split, leg-2 null, leg-3 null) drawn after this amendment is hashed, and a fresh split** — drawn material is never reused from the halted run or from the design stage.
