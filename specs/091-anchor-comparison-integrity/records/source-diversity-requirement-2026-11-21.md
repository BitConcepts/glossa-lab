# Source-diversity requirement — sourcing flags and the promotion rule for the strict-core standings

Filed 2026-11-21 under the owner's order of that day (Glossa-Lab chat,
weekend iteration on the independent audit of the 94 strict-core
readings). The audit's sourcing inventory found 88 of the 94 readings
sourced to a single named source, a single linguistic tradition, or no
checkable linguistic source at all (75 flagged S, 13 flagged N, 5
flagged M, 1 flagged A), and its consequences ruling held that the
strongest sourcing problems are missing sign-to-word derivations,
unverified primary citations, reused or truncated corroboration, and
unexamined dependence. This record turns that finding into a standing
program rule. It changes no standing, re-adjudicates no reading, and
alters no other record.

## The flags

Every reading's record carries a sourcing flag in the audit's scheme:

- **S** — a single named substantive source, or one recorded
  linguistic tradition. An internal phase identifier is a provenance
  pointer, not an extra linguistic authority.
- **N** — no checkable linguistic source is filed; at most an internal
  source pointer is recorded. This is a stronger documentation deficit
  than an identifiable sole authority.
- **M** — more than one tradition or source is expressly recorded.
  This does not by itself establish independence, a correct
  reconstruction, or a valid sign mapping.
- **A** — two catalogue or literature attributions are named, but
  independent linguistic sourcing is not demonstrated.

The flag is recorded alongside the reading's other record fields
whenever a reading is filed, promoted, or revalidated.

## The promotion rule

1. No reading may be promoted to any positive standing on a single
   named source alone, or on a single linguistic tradition alone.
   Promotion requires at least one of:
   - a second source or tradition that is independent of the first
     (a different publication, by a different hand or line of work,
     whose argument does not rest on the first source or on the
     program's own assumptions); or
   - a value-discriminating test result: a test, run under the
     program's ordinary discipline, whose outcome would differ under
     the proposed reading and under at least one named alternative,
     and which came out in the proposed reading's favour.
2. Counted mentions of one publication are one source. Four mentions
   of a single work are a single source, however many evidence lines
   they are filed under.
3. Entries within the one dictionary are one tradition. Two or more
   entries from the same etymological dictionary, cited for one
   reading or across its corroboration lines, count as the single
   tradition that dictionary represents.
4. A crosswalk or catalogue correspondence establishes sign identity
   at most. It is not a second source for a reading's sound or
   meaning.

## What the flags do not do

The flags are documentation, not grades. An S flag alone never
demotes a reading, and an M flag alone never promotes one. A single
language family is not intrinsically weak evidence, and two families
are not intrinsically strong: resemblance can reflect chance,
borrowing, or selective matching. What the rule governs is the act
of promotion: a positive standing may not rest on one source or one
tradition by itself, whatever flag the reading carries.

## Scope

The rule applies from filing to every promotion and to any future
revalidation of an existing standing. A revalidation that would keep
or restore a positive standing is a promotion for the purposes of
this rule and must meet it on the evidence then filed. Readings
already standing are not moved by this record; if a future
revalidation finds that a standing cannot meet the rule, that
finding is reported to the adjudication record, which is where
standings move.
